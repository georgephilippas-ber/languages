## über den (eigenen) Tellerrand hinausblicken / hinausschauen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** To look beyond one's own narrow field, interests, or habits: to take a broader view, consider other
perspectives, disciplines, or countries, and learn from them. Often negated to criticise someone as blinkered: *Er
blickt nie über den Tellerrand hinaus*.

**Synonym:** **den eigenen Horizont erweitern / über den eigenen Horizont hinausdenken / offen für Neues sein**;
**antonym: Scheuklappen tragen (to wear blinkers) / betriebsblind sein (blind from routine)**

**Grammar:** The verb is separable: **blickt über den Tellerrand hinaus – blickte hinaus – hat hinausgeblickt**;
zu-infinitive **hinauszublicken**. **über** takes the **accusative** here (direction): **über den Tellerrand**. The verb
varies freely: **hinausblicken, hinausschauen, hinaussehen, hinausdenken**, colloquially **gucken**. The field can be
added in the genitive: *über den Tellerrand des eigenen Fachs*. Very common as a noun phrase: **der Blick über den
Tellerrand** (*ein Blick über den Tellerrand*, also a typical title for columns and events).

**Example:** *In der Forschung lohnt es sich, über den Tellerrand des eigenen Fachs hinauszublicken.* — "In research,
it pays to look beyond the confines of your own discipline."

Another example: *Ein Blick über den Tellerrand zeigt, dass andere Länder das Problem längst gelöst haben.* — "A look
beyond our own borders shows that other countries solved the problem long ago."

**English:** **to look beyond one's own backyard / broaden one's horizons / take a wider view / think outside the
box**; negated: **to be blinkered / not see beyond the end of one's nose** · **French:** **voir plus loin que le bout
de son nez / élargir ses horizons / sortir des sentiers battus / regarder au-delà de son pré carré**

Useful nuance: **der Tellerrand** is the rim of a plate: the image is of someone who sees only what is on their own
plate. The idiom is about breadth of perspective rather than creativity, so it is closer to "look beyond one's own
backyard" than to "think outside the box". It is very frequent in business, academic, and journalistic German and
can sound slightly clichéd. Its opposites are **Scheuklappen tragen** (narrow-mindedness) and **betriebsblind sein**
(no longer noticing problems because of routine). Don't confuse it with **ein Brett vor dem Kopf haben**, which means
failing to see something obvious.
