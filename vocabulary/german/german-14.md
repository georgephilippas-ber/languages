## übrigen (übrig / die übrigen / im Übrigen)

**CEFR:** roughly **B1** (*übrig*, *die übrigen*); **B2** (*im Übrigen*).

**Definition:** Remaining / the rest / the other(s) — inflected form of **übrig** ("left over, remaining"),
used for what is left once something has been taken, mentioned, or dealt with (*die übrigen Gäste*, "the
remaining/other guests"); in the fixed phrase **im Übrigen** it means "besides / moreover / incidentally /
apart from that."

**Synonym:** **restlich** (remaining, closest for the adjective sense) · **ander-** (other, more neutral) ·
**außerdem / übrigens** (besides / by the way, for *im Übrigen*) depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective **übrig**: predicative (*Es ist nichts übrig*, "Nothing is left") or attributive with
normal adjective endings (*die übrigen Teilnehmer*, *das übrige Geld*). Nominalized: **die Übrigen** ("the
others/the rest"), **das Übrige** ("the rest"). Fixed phrase **im Übrigen** (capitalized since the spelling
reform; *im übrigen* is old spelling) = "moreover / for the rest / incidentally." Related: **übrigens** ("by
the way"), **übrig bleiben** ("to be left over"), **übrig lassen** ("to leave over"), idiom **etwas übrig
haben für** ("to have a soft spot for"), **zu wünschen übrig lassen** ("to leave something to be desired").

**Example:** *Zwei Teilnehmer kamen pünktlich, die übrigen verspäteten sich.* — "Two participants arrived on
time; the others were late."

Another example: *Der Vorschlag ist teuer. Im Übrigen glaube ich nicht, dass er funktioniert.* — "The proposal
is expensive. Besides, I don't think it will work."

**English:** **remaining / the rest / the other(s); (im Übrigen) besides, moreover** · **French:** **restant /
les autres; (im Übrigen) du reste, par ailleurs**

Useful nuance: **Im Übrigen** and **übrigens** look alike but differ: **übrigens** is a casual "by the way"
that introduces a new, often unrelated point, while **im Übrigen** is more formal and means "apart from that
/ furthermore," adding a further argument along the same line — close to French **du reste / au
demeurant**. For the adjective, **die übrigen** stresses "the rest of a group already partly mentioned,"
where **die anderen** is more neutral.

## Beteiligten (der/die Beteiligte)

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** The parties involved / participants / stakeholders — the people (or organizations) who take
part in, are involved in, or are affected by a matter, process, event, or decision; nominalized from the
participle **beteiligt** ("involved").

**Synonym:** **die Teilnehmer** (participants, esp. in an event or course) · **die Betroffenen** (those
affected, more passive) · **die Parteien** (the parties, legal/negotiation context) · **die Mitwirkenden**
(contributors, collaborators) depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjectival noun from the past participle of **sich beteiligen** ("to take part"), so it declines
like an adjective: **der Beteiligte / ein Beteiligter**, **die Beteiligte / eine Beteiligte**, plural **die
Beteiligten / Beteiligte** (no article). Dative/genitive plural: **den/der Beteiligten**. Related: **beteiligt
sein an + Dat.** ("to be involved in"), **sich beteiligen an + Dat.** ("to take part in, participate in"),
**die Beteiligung** ("participation; stake/share"). Common collocations: **alle Beteiligten** ("everyone
involved"), **die beteiligten Parteien** ("the parties involved"), **am Unfall Beteiligte** ("those involved
in the accident").

**Example:** *Vor der Verhandlung sollte man die Interessen aller Beteiligten kennen.* — "Before the
negotiation, one should know the interests of all parties involved."

Another example: *Die Polizei befragte die am Unfall Beteiligten.* — "The police questioned those involved in
the accident."

**English:** **those involved / the parties involved / participants / stakeholders** · **French:** **les
parties prenantes / les intéressés / les personnes concernées**

Useful nuance: **Beteiligte** emphasizes active involvement — having a part or a stake in something — while
**Betroffene** are those affected, possibly without any active role. In business and negotiation German,
**alle Beteiligten** is the standard way to say "all stakeholders," often a more natural choice than the
anglicism **Stakeholder**. Watch the adjectival declension: *ein Beteiligter*, but *der Beteiligte*.

## Schweigen (das Schweigen / schweigen)

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** Silence — the act or state of not speaking, especially deliberately (keeping quiet, refusing
to comment, or holding one's tongue); as a verb, **schweigen** means "to be/remain silent, to say nothing."

**Synonym:** **die Stille** (silence/quiet as the absence of noise, not of speech) · **die Ruhe** (calm,
quiet, peace) · **verstummen** (to fall silent, verb) · **den Mund halten** (to keep one's mouth shut,
colloquial) depending on context

**Grammar:** Noun: **das Schweigen** (neuter, nominalized infinitive, no plural). Verb: strong, intransitive:
**schweigen, schwieg, hat geschwiegen**. Prepositions: **schweigen zu + Dat.** ("to say nothing about/in
response to"), **über etwas (Akk.) schweigen** ("to keep silent about something"). Related: **schweigsam**
("taciturn, quiet"), **verschweigen** ("to conceal, keep quiet about," transitive), **die Schweigepflicht**
("duty of confidentiality"), **die Schweigeminute** ("minute of silence"). Common collocations: **das
Schweigen brechen** ("to break the silence"), **sich in Schweigen hüllen** ("to wrap oneself in silence,
refuse to comment"), **eisiges Schweigen** ("icy silence"), **zum Schweigen bringen** ("to silence").

**Example:** *Nach seinem Angebot ließ sie bewusst ein langes Schweigen entstehen.* — "After his offer, she
deliberately let a long silence develop."

Another example: *Der Minister schwieg zu den Vorwürfen.* — "The minister said nothing in response to the
allegations."

**English:** **silence / to be silent** · **French:** **le silence / se taire**

Useful nuance: **Schweigen** is silence as the *absence of speech* — a human act, often deliberate and
meaningful — whereas **Stille** is the *absence of sound* (a quiet forest, a still night). So: *ein
peinliches Schweigen* ("an awkward silence" in a conversation) but *die Stille der Nacht*. Proverb: **Reden
ist Silber, Schweigen ist Gold** ("Speech is silver, silence is golden") — very apt for negotiation, where
strategic silence after an offer or a label is a key tool.

## darüber hinaus

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Furthermore / moreover / in addition / beyond that — a connective adverb that adds a further
point on top of what was just said; also used literally for going "beyond" a limit or amount (*darüber hinaus
gehen*, "to go beyond that").

**Synonym:** **außerdem** (besides, more everyday) · **zudem** (moreover, slightly formal) · **ferner**
(furthermore, formal/written) · **im Übrigen** (apart from that, for the rest) · **überdies** (moreover,
elevated) depending on context

**Grammar:** Two-word adverbial phrase, written separately: **darüber hinaus** (from **darüber** + **hinaus**,
"out beyond that"). As a connective it takes the first position and triggers verb–subject inversion:
*Darüber hinaus **stehen** sie ...* — or sits in the middle field after the verb: *Sie **stehen** darüber
hinaus ...* ("They are, in addition, ..."). Literal use: **über etwas (Akk.) hinausgehen** ("to exceed, go
beyond"), so *Das geht darüber hinaus* = "That goes beyond it." Don't confuse with **über etwas (Dat.)
stehen / darüber stehen** ("to be above something, not let it get to you").

**Example:** *Gute Verhandlungsführer sind gut vorbereitet. Darüber hinaus stehen sie Überraschungen offen
gegenüber.* — "Good negotiators are well prepared. Furthermore, they are open to surprises."

Another example: *Die Kosten gehen weit darüber hinaus, was wir geplant hatten.* — "The costs go far beyond
what we had planned."

**English:** **furthermore / moreover / in addition / beyond that** · **French:** **en outre / de plus /
au-delà**

Useful nuance: **Darüber hinaus** is the go-to "furthermore" of written and professional German — more formal
than **außerdem**, less stiff than **ferner** or **überdies**. Its literal image ("out beyond that") makes it
especially fitting when the added point goes *further* than the previous one, rather than merely sitting
alongside it. Remember inversion when it opens a clause: *Darüber hinaus **ist** er ...*, never *Darüber
hinaus er ist ...*.

## außerhalb

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** Outside (of) / beyond — a preposition or adverb for something located outside a place, area, or
boundary (*außerhalb der Stadt*, "outside the city"), or falling outside a period of time or a domain
(*außerhalb der Öffnungszeiten*, "outside opening hours"; *außerhalb meiner Zuständigkeit*, "outside my
responsibility").

**Synonym:** **draußen** (outside, adverb only, more everyday/physical) · **jenseits** (beyond, elevated) ·
**nicht innerhalb** depending on context; **antonym: innerhalb** (inside, within)

**Grammar:** Preposition with the **genitive**: **außerhalb + Genitiv** (*außerhalb des Hauses*, *außerhalb
der Saison*). Before names, or nouns without an article, use **von + Dativ**: *außerhalb von Berlin*,
*außerhalb von Europa*. Without a noun, it works as an adverb: *Wir wohnen etwas außerhalb* ("We live a bit
outside [of town]"); *von außerhalb kommen* ("to come from out of town"). In spoken German you may hear the
dative (*außerhalb dem Haus*), but the genitive is the standard form.

**Example:** *Außerhalb der Geschäftszeiten ist der Kundendienst nicht erreichbar.* — "Customer service cannot be
reached outside business hours."

Another example: *Viele Familien ziehen nach außerhalb, weil die Mieten in der Stadt zu hoch sind.* — "Many
families move out of town because rents in the city are too high."

**English:** **outside (of) / beyond / out of town** · **French:** **en dehors de / à l'extérieur de / hors de**

Useful nuance: **außerhalb** marks a *boundary*: something is outside a defined area, time, or scope. It pairs
naturally with abstract nouns: *außerhalb der Arbeitszeit*, *außerhalb der Reichweite* ("out of reach"),
*außerhalb jeder Kontrolle* ("beyond all control"). **draußen** is just "outdoors / out there" with no reference
point: *Die Kinder spielen draußen*, not *außerhalb*. Mirror pair: **innerhalb + Genitiv** ("within"), which is
also used for time limits: *innerhalb einer Woche* ("within a week").
