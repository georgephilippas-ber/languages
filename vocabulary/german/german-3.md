## die Ausgewogenheit

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** Balance or equilibrium — the quality of being well-proportioned, fair, or evenly weighted, in
the sense of a diet, a report, an argument, a portfolio, or a person's temperament containing no excessive or
one-sided element.

**Synonym:** **das Gleichgewicht / die Balance / die Objektivität (for reporting/argument)** depending on
context

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Ausgewogenheit**; usually uncountable, no plural. Derived from the adjective
**ausgewogen** ("balanced"), itself the past participle of the separable verb **etwas (Akkusativ) auswiegen**
("to weigh out/counterbalance"). Common constructions: **auf Ausgewogenheit achten** ("to pay attention to
balance"), **die Ausgewogenheit der Berichterstattung** ("the balance of the reporting"), **die Ausgewogenheit
einer Ernährung** ("the balance of a diet").

**Example:** *Die Redaktion legt großen Wert auf die Ausgewogenheit ihrer Berichterstattung.* — "The editorial
team places great importance on the balance of its reporting."

Another example: *Eine gute Ausgewogenheit zwischen Arbeit und Freizeit ist wichtig für die Gesundheit.* — "A
good balance between work and leisure is important for health."

**English:** **balance / equilibrium / even-handedness** · **French:** **équilibre / impartialité**

Useful nuance: **Ausgewogenheit** is often used for something that should fairly represent multiple sides or
elements — a balanced diet, balanced media coverage, a balanced argument — with a connotation of deliberate
fairness or proportion, closer to "even-handedness" than the more general **Gleichgewicht**, which is the
standard word for "equilibrium" in physical, ecological, or political contexts (*das ökologische
Gleichgewicht*, "the ecological balance").

## durchsetzen / sich (Akkusativ) durchsetzen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To enforce, push through, or successfully implement something, such as a demand, plan, law, or
one's will (**etwas durchsetzen**); reflexively, to prevail, assert oneself, or gain acceptance — said of a
person, idea, technology, or trend that wins out against resistance or competition (**sich durchsetzen**).

**Synonym:** **etwas durchsetzen: erzwingen / durchdrücken / durchbringen**; **sich durchsetzen: sich behaupten
/ Erfolg haben / sich etablieren** depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable weak verb: **durchsetzen – setzte durch – hat durchgesetzt**. Transitive: **etwas
(Akkusativ) [gegen jemanden/etwas (Akkusativ)] durchsetzen** (*seine Forderungen gegen den Widerstand
durchsetzen*, "to push one's demands through against resistance"). Reflexive: **sich (Akkusativ) [gegen
jemanden/etwas (Akkusativ)] durchsetzen** (*sich gegen die Konkurrenz durchsetzen*, "to prevail over the
competition"). The prefix separates in a main clause: *Er setzt sich durch*; infinitive with **zu** is
**durchzusetzen**. Note: an inseparable, differently stressed verb **durchsétzen** (past participle
**durchsetzt**, not **durchgesetzt**) exists with the unrelated meaning "to permeate/intersperse something
with something" (*ein Gestein, das mit Quarz durchsetzt ist*, "rock interspersed with quartz") — a different
word from the same root, distinguished by stress and by its participle.

**Example:** *Die Gewerkschaft konnte ihre Forderungen nach höheren Löhnen durchsetzen.* — "The union was able
to push through its demands for higher wages."

Another example: *Das neue Modell hat sich schnell auf dem Markt durchgesetzt.* — "The new model quickly
prevailed/caught on in the market."

**English:** **to enforce / to push through / to implement**; **sich durchsetzen: to prevail / to assert
oneself / to win out** · **French:** **imposer / faire valoir**; **sich durchsetzen: s'imposer / l'emporter**

Useful nuance: **Durchsetzen** (transitive) emphasizes successfully making something happen despite obstacles
or opposition — a policy, a demand, one's will. **Sich durchsetzen** (reflexive) shifts the focus to a person,
idea, or product itself winning out or gaining acceptance, often in competition with alternatives. Don't
confuse either with **sich einsetzen [für etwas]** ("to advocate/campaign for something"), which describes
effort toward a goal without necessarily implying success.

## jemanden von etwas (Dativ) abbringen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To dissuade or talk someone out of doing something, or to divert someone from a plan, opinion,
or course of action, usually through persuasion or argument.

**Synonym:** **jemanden von etwas überzeugen, es nicht zu tun / jemandem etwas ausreden / jemanden umstimmen**
depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable strong verb: **abbringen – brachte ab – hat abgebracht**. Construction: **jemanden
(Akkusativ) von etwas (Dativ) abbringen** (*ihn von seinem Plan abbringen*, "to talk him out of his plan").
Often used in the negative to express that someone cannot be dissuaded: **sich von etwas nicht abbringen
lassen** ("to not let oneself be talked out of something"). The prefix separates in a main clause: *Sie bringt
ihn davon ab*; infinitive with **zu** is **abzubringen**.

**Example:** *Seine Freunde konnten ihn nicht von seinem Vorhaben abbringen.* — "His friends could not talk him
out of his plan."

Another example: *Sie ließ sich von niemandem von ihrer Meinung abbringen.* — "She would not let anyone talk
her out of her opinion."

**English:** **to dissuade / to talk out of / to divert from** · **French:** **dissuader / détourner de**

Useful nuance: **Abbringen** always specifies what someone is being diverted *from* with **von + Dativ** — a
plan, a decision, an opinion, a path. The common construction **sich nicht abbringen lassen** ("to not be
dissuaded/deterred") emphasizes stubborn resolve. It contrasts with **überreden** ("to persuade someone *to*
do something"), which points the other direction.

## bedächtig

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Deliberate, careful, and unhurried in manner, thought, speech, or movement — thinking things
through before acting or speaking, often at a measured, slow pace. Depending on context it can sound either
positive (thoughtful, prudent) or mildly critical (overly slow, ponderous).

**Synonym:** **besonnen / überlegt / bedachtsam / langsam und überlegt** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective, also used adverbially: *eine bedächtige Antwort*, *Er sprach bedächtig*. Regular
inflection before a noun according to case, gender, number, and determiner: **bedächtig, bedächtiger, am
bedächtigsten**. Related to the verb **bedenken** ("to consider, think over") and the adjective **bedacht**
("mindful, careful," often in **auf etwas (Akkusativ) bedacht sein**, "to be intent on/mindful of something").

**Example:** *Der alte Mann ging langsam und bedächtig über den Hof.* — "The old man walked slowly and
deliberately across the yard."

Another example: *Sie antwortete bedächtig, nachdem sie kurz nachgedacht hatte.* — "She answered thoughtfully,
after considering briefly."

**English:** **deliberate / thoughtful / measured / unhurried** · **French:** **posé / réfléchi / mesuré**

Useful nuance: **Bedächtig** emphasizes a slow, considered manner — someone who thinks before acting or
speaking, whether admiringly ("prudent, careful") or with a hint of impatience ("ponderous, slow"). **Besonnen**
leans more purely positive, stressing level-headedness and composure, especially in a crisis, while
**bedächtig** more strongly evokes physical or verbal slowness alongside the thoughtfulness.

## jemandem etwas (Akkusativ) entziehen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** To withdraw, take away, or revoke something from someone — a right, license, privilege, trust,
or, as in the sample phrase **um ihm seine Verantwortung zu entziehen** ("in order to relieve/strip him of his
responsibility"), a duty or area of authority. Reflexively, **sich (Dativ) etwas entziehen** or **sich einer
Sache (Dativ) entziehen** means to withdraw oneself from something, to evade or escape it (a duty, judgment,
someone's grasp or view).

**Synonym:** **jemandem etwas wegnehmen / jemandem etwas aberkennen / jemandem etwas nehmen** depending on
context; reflexively **sich einer Sache entziehen: sich einer Sache entwinden / sich einer Sache
entziehen/ausweichen**

**Grammar:** Strong, inseparable verb: **entziehen – entzog – hat entzogen**. Construction: **jemandem
(Dativ) etwas (Akkusativ) entziehen** (*ihm seine Verantwortung entziehen*, "to relieve him of his
responsibility"; *jemandem die Fahrerlaubnis entziehen*, "to revoke someone's driving license"). Reflexive:
**sich (Dativ) etwas (Dativ) entziehen** (*sich der Verantwortung entziehen*, "to evade responsibility"; *sich
jemandes Blicken entziehen*, "to withdraw from someone's gaze"). Related noun: **der Entzug** ("withdrawal,"
also used for medical detox: *der Drogenentzug*).

**Example:** *Der Vorstand beschloss, ihm seine Verantwortung zu entziehen und sie einem anderen Kollegen zu
übertragen.* — "The board decided to relieve him of his responsibility and hand it to another colleague."

Another example: *Sie versuchte, sich ihrer Verantwortung zu entziehen, indem sie die Schuld auf andere
schob.* — "She tried to evade her responsibility by shifting the blame onto others."

**English:** **to withdraw / to take away / to revoke / to deprive of**; reflexively **to evade / to escape /
to withdraw from** · **French:** **retirer / priver de / soustraire**; **se soustraire à**

Useful nuance: The dative person and accusative thing pattern (**jemandem etwas entziehen**) marks who loses
something and what is taken; it's the standard verb for formally revoking a license, right, or area of
authority. The reflexive **sich einer Sache entziehen** flips the direction — the subject actively withdraws
from or dodges an obligation, scrutiny, or grasp, closer to "evade" than to being deprived of something.

## der Einsatz

**CEFR:** roughly **B1–B2**.

**Definition:** Effort, dedication, or commitment put into an activity — as in the sample phrase **mit vollem
Einsatz dabei sein** ("to be fully committed/giving it one's all"). The word has several other common senses:
the deployment or use of resources, personnel, or equipment (**der Einsatz von Personal**, "the deployment of
staff"); a mission or operation, especially military or emergency-service (**im Einsatz sein**, "to be on
duty/deployed"); a stake or bet in gambling; and, in music, the entrance or cue for a voice or instrument to
begin.

**Synonym:** **effort/commitment sense: die Hingabe / das Engagement / die Anstrengung**; **deployment sense:
die Verwendung / der Gebrauch**; **mission sense: der Auftrag / die Mission** depending on context

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Einsatz**; plural: **die Einsätze**. Derived from the separable verb
**etwas (Akkusativ) einsetzen** ("to deploy, use, insert") and reflexive **sich (Akkusativ) [für jemanden/etwas
(Akkusativ)] einsetzen** ("to commit oneself/advocate for someone or something"). Common constructions: **mit
vollem Einsatz** ("with full commitment/effort"), **im Einsatz sein** ("to be deployed/on duty"), **seinen
Einsatz zeigen** ("to show one's commitment"), and **unter Einsatz seines Lebens** ("at the risk of one's
life").

**Example:** *Die Mannschaft war den ganzen Abend mit vollem Einsatz dabei.* — "The team was fully committed
all evening, giving it everything."

Another example: *Die Feuerwehr war schon wenige Minuten nach dem Alarm im Einsatz.* — "The fire department
was already on the scene/deployed just a few minutes after the alarm."

**English:** **effort / commitment / dedication**; also **deployment / use / mission / stake (gambling) /
entrance (music)** · **French:** **engagement / dévouement**; aussi **déploiement / usage / mission / mise**

Useful nuance: In the phrase **mit vollem Einsatz**, **Einsatz** describes the visible energy and dedication
someone puts into a task or competition, similar to English "giving it your all." This sense is closely tied
to the reflexive verb **sich einsetzen** ("to commit oneself to something") — someone who shows **Einsatz** is
someone who **sich einsetzt**. Context determines which of the word's several meanings (effort, deployment,
stake, musical entrance) applies.

## der Nachschub

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Supplies, reinforcements, or fresh stock brought in to replenish what has been used up —
originally and still commonly a military term for the resupply of troops with matériel, ammunition, or
provisions, but also used more broadly and colloquially for replenishing any resource (food, goods, coffee,
etc.).

**Synonym:** **die Versorgung / der Nachschubdienst / die Nachlieferung** depending on context

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Nachschub**; usually uncountable, no plural. Derived from the separable
verb **etwas (Akkusativ) nachschieben** ("to push/supply something afterward"). Common constructions:
**Nachschub an etwas (Dativ)** (*Nachschub an Munition*, "a resupply of ammunition"), **Nachschub bekommen/
brauchen** ("to get/need fresh supplies"), and **für Nachschub sorgen** ("to see to resupply/restocking").
Related to [[der Tross]] (a baggage/supply train).

**Example:** *Die eingeschlossenen Truppen warteten dringend auf Nachschub an Lebensmitteln und Munition.* —
"The besieged troops were urgently waiting for a resupply of food and ammunition."

Another example: *Kannst du beim Einkaufen gleich Nachschub an Kaffee besorgen?* — "Can you pick up more
coffee while you're shopping?"

**English:** **supplies / resupply / reinforcements / fresh stock** · **French:** **ravitaillement / réserves**

Useful nuance: **Nachschub** keeps a military or logistical flavor even in its colloquial extensions — using
it for coffee or snacks running low has a slightly jokey, mock-serious tone, as if treating the household
supply like a military resupply operation. **Versorgung** is the more neutral, general word for "supply/
provisioning."

## die Mäßigung

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** Moderation or restraint — the quality or act of keeping something (behavior, consumption,
emotions, demands) within reasonable, measured limits rather than excessive or extreme; also used in
political/diplomatic contexts for a policy of restraint or conciliation toward an opposing side.

**Synonym:** **die Zurückhaltung / die Beherrschung / das Maßhalten** depending on context; opposite: **die
Maßlosigkeit / der Exzess**

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Mäßigung**; usually uncountable, no plural. Derived from the verb **etwas
(Akkusativ) mäßigen** ("to moderate/temper something") and reflexive **sich (Akkusativ) mäßigen** ("to
restrain/moderate oneself"). Common constructions: **zur Mäßigung aufrufen** ("to call for restraint/
moderation"), **Mäßigung üben** ("to exercise restraint"), and **mit Mäßigung** ("with moderation").

**Example:** *Die Regierung rief beide Seiten zur Mäßigung auf.* — "The government called on both sides to
exercise restraint."

Another example: *Er isst und trinkt immer mit Mäßigung.* — "He always eats and drinks in moderation."

**English:** **moderation / restraint / temperance** · **French:** **modération / retenue**

Useful nuance: **Mäßigung** often appears in political and diplomatic register (**zur Mäßigung aufrufen**,
calling on parties in a conflict to show restraint), giving it a more formal, public tone than the everyday
**Zurückhaltung** ("restraint/reserve," more personal). The related reflexive verb **sich mäßigen** is common
in everyday advice, e.g. telling someone to calm down or moderate their behavior.

## maßvoll

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** Moderate, measured, or restrained — describing behavior, demands, tone, or actions that stay
within sensible, appropriate limits rather than being excessive, extreme, or exaggerated.

**Synonym:** **gemäßigt / zurückhaltend / besonnen** depending on context; opposite: **maßlos / übertrieben /
exzessiv**

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **maßvoll, maßvolle, maßvoller, maßvolles**. Formed from **das Maß**
("measure/moderation") + **-voll** ("full of"), literally "full of measure." Common constructions: **maßvolle
Kritik** ("measured criticism"), **maßvoll reagieren** ("to react moderately/with restraint"), and **eine
maßvolle Lohnerhöhung** ("a moderate pay raise").

**Example:** *Die Opposition übte maßvolle Kritik an der Reform.* — "The opposition offered measured criticism
of the reform."

Another example: *Er reagierte auch in der hitzigen Debatte maßvoll und sachlich.* — "Even in the heated
debate, he reacted moderately and matter-of-factly."

**English:** **moderate / measured / restrained** · **French:** **modéré / mesuré**

Useful nuance: **maßvoll** is closely related to [[die Mäßigung]] and the verb **sich mäßigen** — where
**Mäßigung** names the quality or act of restraint, **maßvoll** describes something (a reaction, a demand, a
tone) that displays that quality. It's often used approvingly, especially in political or journalistic
writing, to praise a response as sensible and proportionate rather than inflammatory.

## die Anstrengung

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Effort or exertion — the physical or mental energy expended to achieve something difficult or
demanding; can refer to a single effort/endeavor or, in the plural, to sustained efforts/pains taken toward a
goal.

**Synonym:** **die Mühe / der Aufwand / die Bemühung** depending on context; intensified: **die Kraftanstrengung**
("major effort/exertion")

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Anstrengung**; plural: **die Anstrengungen**. Derived from the reflexive verb
**sich (Akkusativ) anstrengen** ("to exert oneself, make an effort") and transitive **etwas (Akkusativ)
anstrengen** ("to strain/tax something," e.g. **die Augen anstrengen**, "to strain one's eyes"). Common
constructions: **Anstrengungen unternehmen** ("to make efforts"), **unter großer Anstrengung** ("with great
effort/exertion"), and **es ist der Anstrengung wert** ("it's worth the effort").

**Example:** *Trotz aller Anstrengungen konnte die Mannschaft die Niederlage nicht verhindern.* — "Despite all
efforts, the team could not prevent the defeat."

Another example: *Das lange Bergwandern war eine echte körperliche Anstrengung.* — "The long mountain hike was
a real physical exertion."

**English:** **effort / exertion / strain** · **French:** **effort / peine**

Useful nuance: **Anstrengung** leans toward the effortful, taxing side of exertion — physical strain, mental
concentration, or sustained endeavor — and pairs naturally with **groß, körperlich, geistig** ("great,
physical, mental"). It differs from [[der Einsatz]], which emphasizes visible commitment/dedication (as in
sport or duty) rather than the sheer exertion or strain involved.

## der Eifer

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** Zeal, ardor, or eagerness — enthusiastic, energetic dedication to a task, cause, or goal, often
with a connotation of impatience or excessive keenness; can be positive (diligent enthusiasm) or slightly
critical (overzealousness).

**Synonym:** **die Begeisterung / der Elan / die Inbrunst** depending on context; overzealous sense: **der
Übereifer**

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Eifer**; uncountable, no plural. Related adjective: **eifrig** ("eager,
zealous, diligent"). Common constructions: **mit Eifer bei der Sache sein** ("to be zealously engaged in
something"), **im Eifer des Gefechts** ("in the heat of the moment," literally "in the zeal of battle"), and
**blinder Eifer** ("blind zeal," i.e. overenthusiasm that does more harm than good).

**Example:** *Sie stürzte sich mit großem Eifer in die neue Aufgabe.* — "She threw herself into the new task
with great zeal."

Another example: *Im Eifer des Gefechts hatte er ganz vergessen, die Tür abzuschließen.* — "In the heat of the
moment, he had completely forgotten to lock the door."

**English:** **zeal / ardor / eagerness / fervor** · **French:** **zèle / ardeur / empressement**

Useful nuance: **Eifer** sits close to [[die Anstrengung]] and [[der Einsatz]] but names the emotional
drive/enthusiasm behind an effort rather than the exertion or commitment itself. The idiom **im Eifer des
Gefechts** is very common for describing something said or done hastily amid excitement or heated activity,
and **blinder Eifer** captures the specifically German-flavored caution that overzealousness can backfire —
echoed in the proverb "blinder Eifer schadet nur" ("blind zeal only does harm").

## sich davon abhalten

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To keep oneself from (doing) something, to refrain — the reflexive counterpart of **jemanden
davon abhalten** ("to keep/prevent someone from doing something"); **davon** is a pronominal adverb pointing
forward or back to an infinitive clause (**..., etwas zu tun**), so **sich davon abhalten** literally means "to
hold oneself back from that [action]."

**Synonym:** **sich zurückhalten / sich etwas verkneifen / auf etwas verzichten** depending on context; opposite
construction: **sich nicht abhalten lassen** ("to not let oneself be stopped/deterred")

**Grammar:** Separable strong verb: **abhalten, hielt ab, hat abgehalten**. Base transitive construction:
**jemanden (Akkusativ) von etwas/davon (+ zu-Infinitiv) abhalten** (*ihn davon abhalten, zu gehen*, "to keep him
from leaving"). Reflexive: **sich (Akkusativ) von etwas/davon abhalten [lassen]** — used almost always with a
modal (**lassen, können**) rather than as a bare main verb: **sich davon abhalten lassen** ("to let oneself be
kept/deterred from it") or, in the negative, **sich davon nicht abhalten lassen** ("to not let anything stop
one"). The **davon** stands in for a **zu**-infinitive clause that follows or was just mentioned (*..., davon
abzuhalten, etwas zu tun*). The prefix separates in a main clause: *Sie hält sich davon ab*; infinitive with
**zu** is **abzuhalten**.

**Example:** *Lass dich nicht davon abhalten, deine Ziele zu verfolgen.* — "Don't let yourself be kept from
pursuing your goals."

Another example: *Er konnte sich nicht davon abhalten, ständig aufs Handy zu schauen.* — "He couldn't stop
himself from constantly checking his phone."

**English:** **to keep oneself from / to refrain from / (with lassen) to let oneself be deterred from** ·
**French:** **se retenir de / s'empêcher de**

Useful nuance: In practice, **sich davon abhalten** rarely stands alone as a plain reflexive verb; it almost
always appears with **lassen** (**sich davon abhalten lassen**, "to let oneself be deterred/kept from it"),
especially in the negative imperative or statement of resolve (**lass dich nicht davon abhalten...**, **sie
ließ sich nicht davon abhalten...**). Compare the transitive **jemanden davon abhalten**, where one person
actively stops another — the reflexive shifts this to stopping (or failing to stop) oneself.

## erwecken

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To awaken or arouse — literally, to rouse someone from sleep or, in elevated/literary use, to
bring back to life (**zum Leben erwecken**); much more commonly used figuratively, to evoke or give rise to a
feeling, interest, or impression, especially in the fixed phrase **den Eindruck erwecken, dass...** ("to create/
give the impression that...").

**Synonym:** **wecken** (the everyday, non-figurative counterpart) · **hervorrufen / auslösen** (evoke/trigger,
for feelings or reactions) · **zum Leben erwecken: wiederbeleben** (revive) depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak, inseparable verb: **erwecken, erweckte, hat erweckt**. Governs the accusative: **etwas/
jemanden (Akkusativ) erwecken**. Key fixed constructions: **den Eindruck erwecken, dass...** ("to give the
impression that..."), **Interesse/Aufmerksamkeit erwecken** ("to arouse interest/attention"), **Hoffnungen
erwecken** ("to raise hopes"), **jemanden/etwas zum Leben erwecken** ("to bring someone/something to life,"
literal in fiction/myth or figurative for a project, character, city district, etc.). Related noun: **die
Erweckung** ("awakening," also religious, as in **die Erweckungsbewegung**, "revivalist movement").

**Example:** *Seine Rede erweckte den Eindruck, dass er die Entscheidung bereits getroffen hatte.* — "His speech
gave the impression that he had already made the decision."

Another example: *Die alte Fabrikhalle wurde durch das neue Kulturzentrum wieder zum Leben erweckt.* — "The old
factory hall was brought back to life by the new cultural center."

**English:** **to awaken / arouse / evoke; (den Eindruck erwecken) to give the impression** · **French:**
**éveiller / susciter; donner l'impression**

Useful nuance: **erwecken** sits a register above plain **wecken** ("to wake someone up," e.g. with an alarm
clock) and is reserved for figurative, literary, or elevated contexts — impressions, feelings, interest,
hopes, or a dramatic "return to life." The idiom **den Eindruck erwecken, dass...** is extremely common in
news and formal writing as a slightly more cautious/distancing way to report something than stating it as
fact outright (implying "it seems/appears that," without fully committing).

## verderben / das Verderben

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** As a verb, **verderben** means to spoil or go bad (food, transitively or intransitively), to
ruin or spoil something (a mood, an occasion, a plan), or to corrupt someone morally; the past participle
**verdorben** also functions as an adjective ("spoiled, rotten; morally corrupt/depraved"). As a nominalized
infinitive, **das Verderben** is a more literary/dramatic noun meaning ruin, doom, or perdition — utter
downfall or destruction, often in a fateful sense.

**Synonym:** **verderben (spoil food): schlecht werden / faulen**; **verderben (ruin something): ruinieren /
vermiesen / kaputtmachen**; **verderben (corrupt someone): korrumpieren**; **das Verderben: der Untergang / der
Ruin / das Unheil** depending on context

**Grammar:** Strong, irregular verb: **verderben, verdarb, hat/ist verdorben** — intransitive with **sein** when
describing something going bad itself (*die Milch ist verdorben*, "the milk has gone bad"), transitive with
**haben** when someone/something spoils or ruins something else (*er hat mir den Abend verdorben*, "he ruined
my evening" — dative of the person affected + accusative of the thing ruined). Common fixed constructions:
**jemandem die Laune/den Tag/den Spaß verderben** ("to ruin someone's mood/day/fun"), **sich (Dativ) den Magen
verderben** ("to upset one's stomach"), **es sich mit jemandem verderben** ("to fall out of favor with
someone, spoil one's relationship with someone"). The adjective **verdorben** declines regularly: **verdorbene
Lebensmittel** ("spoiled food"), **ein verdorbener Charakter** ("a corrupt/depraved character"). The nominalized
**das Verderben** is uncountable, usually appearing in phrases like **jemanden ins Verderben stürzen/reißen**
("to plunge/drag someone into ruin") and **sein eigenes Verderben** ("one's own downfall/undoing").

**Example:** *Die Hitze hat das Fleisch schnell verderben lassen.* — "The heat quickly caused the meat to
spoil."

Another example: *Seine Gier stürzte ihn schließlich ins Verderben.* — "His greed ultimately plunged him into
ruin."

**English:** **verderben: to spoil / go bad / ruin; to corrupt; verdorben: spoiled, corrupt/depraved**;
**das Verderben: ruin / doom / perdition** · **French:** **verderben: gâter / pourrir; corrompre**;
**das Verderben: perte / perdition / ruine**

Useful nuance: The everyday senses of **verderben** (spoiled milk, a ruined mood, an upset stomach) feel quite
mundane, but the nominalized **das Verderben** keeps a heightened, almost biblical or tragic register — it's
the word used for a fateful, often self-inflicted downfall (**sein eigenes Verderben**, **ins Verderben
stürzen**), closer to English "doom/perdition" than to the plain verb's "spoil/ruin." Don't confuse **verderben**
with **verderblich** ("perishable," as in **verderbliche Lebensmittel**, "perishable food").

## verleihen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To lend out or rent out something (a physical item, for temporary use, typically for a fee); or,
figuratively, to confer, bestow, or award something abstract — a prize, title, honor, or quality/characteristic
— onto someone or something.

**Synonym:** **lending sense: ausleihen / vermieten (for a fee)**; **conferring sense: vergeben / zuerkennen
(award) / geben (give, more general)** depending on context

**Grammar:** Strong, inseparable verb: **verleihen, verlieh, hat verliehen**. Governs dative of recipient +
accusative of the thing given: **jemandem etwas (Akkusativ) verleihen**. Common fixed constructions: **einen
Preis/Titel/Orden verleihen** ("to award a prize/title/medal"), **einer Sache (Dativ) Ausdruck verleihen** ("to
give expression to something," e.g. a feeling), **jemandem Kraft/Mut verleihen** ("to give someone
strength/courage," often figuratively), and, literally, **Fahrräder/Bücher verleihen** ("to rent out
bikes/lend out books"). Related nouns: **die Verleihung** ("the awarding, presentation ceremony," e.g. **die
Oscar-Verleihung**), and **der Verleih** ("a rental business or distribution agency," e.g. **der
Fahrradverleih**, "bike rental," **der Filmverleih**, "film distribution company").

**Example:** *Der Nobelpreis wird jedes Jahr in Stockholm verliehen.* — "The Nobel Prize is awarded every year
in Stockholm."

Another example: *Die ruhige Musik verleiht dem Film eine besondere Atmosphäre.* — "The calm music lends the
film a special atmosphere."

**English:** **to lend/rent out; to confer/bestow/award** · **French:** **prêter/louer; conférer/décerner**

Useful nuance: The figurative sense (**einer Sache Ausdruck/Kraft/eine Atmosphäre verleihen**) is extremely
common in written and journalistic German for describing how one thing imparts a quality to another — a piece
of music lending a film atmosphere, a gesture lending words emphasis — functioning much like English "lend" in
"lends credibility to." Don't confuse it with **ausleihen** (to borrow/check out, from the borrower's
perspective) — **verleihen** is always from the giver's/lender's side.

## die Besorgnis

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** Concern, worry, or apprehension — a formal noun for unease about a potential negative
development or outcome, often used in news, official, or medical/diagnostic register rather than everyday
speech.

**Synonym:** **die Sorge** (more everyday/general worry) · **die Beunruhigung** (unease, disquiet) · **die
Befürchtung** (a specific fear/apprehension that something will happen) depending on context

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Besorgnis**; plural **die Besorgnisse** (uncommon, mostly used in singular).
Related adjective **besorgt** ("worried, concerned"), and the compound adjective **besorgniserregend**
("alarming, worrying," literally "concern-arousing" — see [[erwecken]]/erregen). Common fixed constructions:
**Anlass zur Besorgnis geben** ("to give cause for concern"), **mit [wachsender] Besorgnis** ("with [growing]
concern"), **Besorgnis äußern** ("to express concern"), **etwas erfüllt jemanden mit Besorgnis** ("something
fills someone with concern").

**Example:** *Der starke Anstieg der Fälle gibt Anlass zur Besorgnis.* — "The sharp rise in cases gives cause
for concern."

Another example: *Die Ärzte äußerten Besorgnis über den Gesundheitszustand des Patienten.* — "The doctors
expressed concern about the patient's state of health."

**English:** **concern / worry / apprehension** · **French:** **inquiétude / préoccupation**

Useful nuance: **Besorgnis** is a notably formal, written-register word — common in news reports, official
statements, and medical contexts (**Anlass zur Besorgnis**, **besorgniserregender Zustand**, "an alarming/
worrying condition") — where everyday spoken German would simply use **Sorge** or **Angst**. The derived
adjective **besorgniserregend** is extremely common in journalism for describing statistics, trends, or
situations that are cause for alarm.

## der Skrupel

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** A scruple or qualm — a moral hesitation or twinge of conscience that holds someone back from
doing something they suspect is wrong; almost always used in the plural, **die Skrupel** ("scruples, qualms"),
especially in the common phrase **keine Skrupel haben** ("to have no scruples, be unscrupulous").

**Synonym:** **die Bedenken** (reservations/misgivings, slightly broader) · **das Gewissen** (conscience, the
underlying faculty) · **die Hemmung** (inhibition) depending on context

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Skrupel**; plural **die Skrupel** (identical form), overwhelmingly used in
the plural. Related adjective: **skrupellos** ("unscrupulous, ruthless") and its opposite **skrupulös**
("scrupulous, overly conscientious," rarer, sometimes with a critical "fussy" connotation). Common fixed
constructions: **[keine] Skrupel haben, etwas zu tun** ("to have [no] qualms about doing something"), **Skrupel
bekommen** ("to develop qualms"), **ohne die geringsten Skrupel** ("without the slightest scruples").

**Example:** *Er hatte keinerlei Skrupel, seine Kollegen zu hintergehen.* — "He had absolutely no scruples
about double-crossing his colleagues."

Another example: *Kurz vor der Tat bekam sie plötzlich Skrupel.* — "Just before the act, she suddenly developed
qualms."

**English:** **scruple(s) / qualm(s)** · **French:** **scrupule(s)**

Useful nuance: **Skrupel** almost never appears in the singular in natural speech — English "a scruple" would
still typically be rendered with the German plural, **Skrupel haben**. The adjective **skrupellos**
("unscrupulous") is far more frequent in everyday and journalistic German than the noun itself, commonly
describing ruthless business practices, criminals, or politicians (**ein skrupelloser Geschäftsmann**).

## bescheren

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To give as a gift, especially in the context of Christmas present-giving; much more broadly and
commonly used figuratively, to bring about or result in something (an outcome, good or bad) for someone —
success, joy, a record, a disappointment, a setback.

**Synonym:** **schenken** (give as a gift, more general/everyday) · **einbringen / bringen / verschaffen**
(figurative "bring about") depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak verb: **bescheren, bescherte, hat beschert**. Governs dative of recipient + accusative of the
thing given/brought about: **jemandem etwas (Akkusativ) bescheren**. Common constructions: **jemandem ein Fest/
Weihnachten bescheren** ("to give someone presents at a celebration/Christmas," a specifically German
Christmas-Eve tradition of gift-giving), and figuratively **jemandem einen Erfolg/eine Niederlage/eine
Überraschung bescheren** ("to bring someone a success/defeat/surprise"). Related noun: **die Bescherung** — the
literal Christmas Eve gift-giving ritual, but also, colloquially and often ironically, "the mess/fiasco" in
the exclamation **Da haben wir die Bescherung!** ("Now we've got a right mess on our hands!").

**Example:** *Der ungewöhnlich warme Winter bescherte den Skigebieten enorme Probleme.* — "The unusually warm
winter brought enormous problems for the ski resorts."

Another example: *Am Heiligabend wird in vielen deutschen Familien schon am Nachmittag beschert.* — "On
Christmas Eve, many German families already exchange presents in the afternoon."

**English:** **to give (as a gift); to bring about (an outcome)** · **French:** **offrir (cadeaux); apporter/
causer (un résultat)**

Useful nuance: Outside the literal Christmas context, **bescheren** is a very common, slightly formal/
journalistic way to say that some event or circumstance "brought" someone a particular outcome — often used
with impersonal subjects like weather, a match, or a decision (*das Spiel bescherte dem Verein den ersten
Titel seit Jahren*, "the match brought the club its first title in years"). The idiom **da haben wir die
Bescherung** wryly repurposes the cheerful Christmas noun to mean the opposite — an unwelcome mess has now
arrived.

## die Abschreckung

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** Deterrence — the act or strategy of discouraging an action (aggression, crime, an attack) by
making its consequences appear too costly or frightening; a key term in military/political and legal contexts
(e.g. nuclear deterrence, deterrent sentencing). Note: spelled **Abschreckung**, with an **c** before the
**k** (not "Abschrekung").

**Synonym:** **die Abschreckungswirkung** (deterrent effect specifically) · in looser contexts **die
Warnung** (warning) depending on context

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Abschreckung**; usually uncountable, no plural. Derived from the separable
verb **jemanden [von etwas] abschrecken** ("to deter/put someone off [something]"; also, in cooking, literally
"to cool something rapidly under cold water," e.g. **die Eier nach dem Kochen abschrecken**, "to rapid-cool the
eggs after boiling"). Common constructions: **nukleare/atomare Abschreckung** ("nuclear deterrence"), **zur
Abschreckung dienen** ("to serve as a deterrent"), **die Politik der Abschreckung** ("the policy of
deterrence"), **abschreckende Wirkung** ("deterrent effect," from the adjective **abschreckend**, which also
means "off-putting, repulsive" in non-military contexts, e.g. *ein abschreckendes Beispiel*, "a cautionary/
deterrent example").

**Example:** *Während des Kalten Krieges beruhte die Sicherheitspolitik beider Blöcke auf nuklearer
Abschreckung.* — "During the Cold War, both blocs' security policy rested on nuclear deterrence."

Another example: *Härtere Strafen sollen zur Abschreckung potenzieller Straftäter beitragen.* — "Harsher
penalties are meant to help deter potential offenders."

**English:** **deterrence** · **French:** **dissuasion**

Useful nuance: **Abschreckung** is the standard term in military/strategic contexts (**nukleare
Abschreckung**, echoing English "nuclear deterrence"/French "dissuasion nucléaire") but the same root verb
**abschrecken** doubles as an everyday cooking term for rapid-cooling boiled food under cold water — an
unrelated, purely literal sense of "shocking/startling [with cold]" that has nothing to do with deterrence.

## umgekehrte Einschüchterung

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1** (compositional phrase, not a fixed idiom).

**Definition:** "Reverse intimidation" — a would-be aggressor being intimidated, deterred, or made to hesitate
themselves, rather than succeeding in intimidating their target; i.e., turning the dynamic of intimidation back
onto the one who tried to impose it. *(Note: correct spelling is **umgekehrte**, from **umgekehrt**, not
"umgehehrte".)* Not a standard fixed idiom — a transparent combination of **umgekehrt** ("reversed, the other
way around") + **die Einschüchterung** ("intimidation"), close in spirit to the idiom [[der Spieß]] **umdrehen**
("to turn the tables").

**Synonym:** **den Spieß umdrehen** (to turn the tables, more idiomatic) · **Abschreckung** (deterrence, when
framed as a strategy rather than a reversal) depending on context

**Grammar:** **umgekehrt** — adjective/adverb, past participle of the separable verb **umkehren** ("to turn
around, reverse"); declines like a normal adjective before a noun (**umgekehrte, umgekehrter, umgekehrtes**) and
is also used adverbially meaning "conversely, vice versa" (**und umgekehrt**, "and vice versa"). **die
Einschüchterung** — feminine noun, plural **die Einschüchterungen**, from the separable weak verb **jemanden
einschüchtern** ("to intimidate, cow, browbeat someone"), itself related to the adjective **schüchtern**
("shy, timid"). Together as a noun phrase: **die umgekehrte Einschüchterung**, or verbally **den Einschüchterer
selbst einschüchtern/abschrecken** ("to intimidate/deter the intimidator himself").

**Example:** *Wer Aggressoren mit umgekehrter Einschüchterung begegnet, nimmt ihnen den Mut zum Angriff.* —
"Meeting aggressors with reverse intimidation takes away their nerve to attack."

Another example: *Statt sich einschüchtern zu lassen, drehte sie den Spieß um und schüchterte ihrerseits den
Angreifer ein.* — "Instead of letting herself be intimidated, she turned the tables and intimidated the
attacker in turn."

**English:** **reverse intimidation** · **French:** **intimidation inversée**

Useful nuance: This phrase isn't a set idiom you'll find in a dictionary — it's a descriptive construction built
from **umgekehrt** + **Einschüchterung**, and a German speaker would more naturally reach for the vivid idiom
**den Spieß umdrehen** ("to turn the tables/turn the weapon around," see [[der Spieß]]) to express the same idea
of flipping intimidation back onto its source. It directly echoes the deterrence logic in [[die Abschreckung]]
and [[jemanden von etwas (Dativ) abbringen]]/[[sich davon abhalten]]: discouraging an aggressor by making the
cost or risk to *them* outweigh the appeal of attacking.

## schlicht

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Plain, simple, or unadorned — describing something (clothing, style, a room, language) that is
modest and free of decoration or complication, often with a positive connotation of tasteful restraint; also
used adverbially as an intensifying particle meaning "simply, plainly, just," especially to flatly assert
something as an unqualified fact.

**Synonym:** **einfach** (simple, more neutral/general) · **schmucklos** (unadorned, more about lack of
decoration) · **bescheiden** (modest) depending on context; intensifier sense: **einfach / rundheraus /
geradezu**

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **schlicht, schlichte, schlichter, schlichtes**. Also used
adverbially before an adjective or verb to mean "simply/just," often doubled in the fixed phrase **schlicht
und einfach** ("plain and simple," "quite simply"): *das ist schlicht und einfach falsch* ("that's simply/
plainly wrong"). Related noun: **die Schlichtheit** ("simplicity, plainness"). Note: not to be confused with
the similarly spelled but unrelated verb **schlichten** ("to mediate/settle a dispute," or literally "to
smooth/plane [wood]"), from a different root.

**Example:** *Sie trug ein schlichtes, aber elegantes Kleid.* — "She wore a plain but elegant dress."

Another example: *Das ist schlicht und einfach nicht wahr.* — "That's simply not true."

**English:** **plain / simple / unadorned; (adverbial) simply/plainly** · **French:** **simple / sobre;
(adverbial) tout simplement**

Useful nuance: As an intensifying adverb, **schlicht** (often as **schlicht und einfach** or **schlichtweg**)
adds a tone of blunt, unqualified assertion — "simply/flatly [true/false/impossible]" — similar to English
"quite simply." As a descriptive adjective, it carries a favorable connotation of tasteful minimalism (a plain
but elegant style) rather than the more neutral or occasionally negative **einfach** ("simple," which can also
mean "basic/unsophisticated").

## kühn

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Bold, daring, or audacious — describing a plan, idea, claim, or action that takes a significant
risk or breaks with convention, often admiringly (courageous, visionary) but sometimes with a hint of
recklessness or presumption. *(Note: **kühnes** is simply the neuter strong-declension ending of **kühn**, as
in **ein kühnes Unterfangen**, "a bold undertaking.")*

**Synonym:** **wagemutig / verwegen / gewagt** (daring/risky) · **mutig** (brave, more general) · **dreist**
(more negative: brazen/presumptuous) depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **kühn, kühne, kühner, kühnes**; comparative/superlative **kühner,
am kühnsten**. Related noun: **die Kühnheit** ("boldness, audacity"). Common collocations: **ein kühner Plan**
("a bold plan"), **eine kühne Behauptung** ("a bold/audacious claim"), **kühne Fantasie** ("bold/vivid
imagination"), **ein kühnes Unterfangen** ("a bold undertaking"), **es wagen, kühn zu sein** ("to dare to be
bold").

**Example:** *Der Architekt entwarf ein kühnes Gebäude aus Glas und Stahl.* — "The architect designed a bold
building of glass and steel."

Another example: *Das war eine kühne Behauptung, die er nicht belegen konnte.* — "That was a bold claim that he
couldn't substantiate."

**English:** **bold / daring / audacious** · **French:** **audacieux / hardi**

Useful nuance: **kühn** sits between admiring **mutig** ("brave," about facing danger or fear) and more
loaded **verwegen**/**dreist** (recklessly daring, or brazen/impudent) — it typically praises boldness of
vision, imagination, or design (a bold plan, a bold architectural choice) rather than physical courage. Context
determines whether it reads as complimentary (visionary) or mildly critical (overreaching).

## der Anschein vs. die Erscheinung

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** **Der Anschein** is the outward impression something gives — how a situation, statement, or
person *seems* to be, often with the implication that this surface impression may not match reality (a
semblance, possibly deceptive). **Die Erscheinung** is broader: a person's physical presence or outward figure
(*eine imposante Erscheinung*, "an imposing figure"), a phenomenon or occurrence (*eine Naturerscheinung*, "a
natural phenomenon"), or a supernatural apparition/vision (*eine Erscheinung haben*, "to have a vision/see an
apparition"); it names the visible/perceptible thing itself, not a possibly-misleading impression about it.

**Synonym:** **Anschein: der Eindruck / die Optik (colloquial)**; **Erscheinung: die Gestalt (figure) / das
Phänomen (occurrence) / die Vision/das Gespenst (apparition sense)** depending on context

**Grammar:** Both masculine/feminine nouns respectively: **der Anschein** (uncountable, no plural, from the verb
**scheinen**, "to seem/shine") and **die Erscheinung** (plural **die Erscheinungen**, from the verb
**erscheinen**, "to appear/be published"). Fixed **Anschein** constructions: **den Anschein erwecken/haben,
dass...** ("to give/have the appearance that..." — see [[erwecken]]), **allem Anschein nach** ("to all
appearances, apparently"), **der Anschein trügt** ("appearances are deceiving"), **zum Schein** (related fixed
phrase, "for show, as a pretense"). Fixed **Erscheinung** constructions: **in Erscheinung treten** ("to appear,
manifest, come to notice," e.g. of symptoms or public figures), **eine imposante/stattliche Erscheinung sein**
("to be an imposing/striking figure/presence"), **eine Erscheinung haben** ("to have a vision, see an
apparition").

**Example:** *Allem Anschein nach hat er die Prüfung bestanden, auch wenn er selbst noch skeptisch ist.* — "To
all appearances he passed the exam, even though he himself is still skeptical."

Another example: *Mit ihrer roten Robe war sie eine auffällige Erscheinung auf der Gala.* — "With her red gown,
she was a striking figure/presence at the gala."

**English:** **Anschein: appearance / semblance / (outward) impression**; **Erscheinung: appearance (physical
presence) / phenomenon / apparition** · **French:** **Anschein: apparence / semblant**; **Erscheinung:
apparition / phénomène / silhouette**

Useful nuance: The key distinction: **Anschein** is always about how something *seems to an observer*, carrying
an implicit "...but maybe isn't really" (*der Anschein trügt*, "appearances deceive"); **Erscheinung** names the
observable thing itself — a person's presence, a physical/natural phenomenon, or (in its more dramatic sense) a
ghostly vision — without that same built-in doubt about truth versus surface. You'd say **den Anschein
erwecken** for "give a false/misleading impression," but **in Erscheinung treten** for "to actually show up/
manifest" — the latter makes no claim about deception.

## der Anschein

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** The outward appearance, semblance, or impression something gives — how a person, situation, or
statement *seems* from the outside, typically with the implication that this surface impression may not
correspond to the underlying reality. Compare [[der Anschein vs. die Erscheinung]].

**Synonym:** **der Eindruck** (impression, more neutral/general) · **der Schein** (semblance/appearance, closely
related, as in **zum Schein**, "for show") depending on context

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Anschein**; uncountable, no plural. From the verb **scheinen** ("to seem,
appear; to shine"). Common fixed constructions: **den Anschein erwecken/haben, dass...** ("to give/have the
appearance that..."; see [[erwecken]]), **allem Anschein nach** ("to all appearances, apparently"), **der
Anschein trügt** ("appearances are deceiving/deceptive"), **sich den Anschein geben** ("to affect an air of
something, put on the appearance of," reflexive and slightly pejorative — implying a deliberate pretense).

**Example:** *Allem Anschein nach ist das Projekt gescheitert, auch wenn niemand es offiziell zugibt.* — "To all
appearances the project has failed, even though nobody admits it officially."

Another example: *Er gab sich den Anschein von Kompetenz, ohne wirklich etwas zu wissen.* — "He put on an air
of competence without actually knowing anything."

**English:** **appearance / semblance / (outward) impression** · **French:** **apparence / semblant**

Useful nuance: **Anschein** almost always carries a latent contrast with reality — using it (rather than the
more neutral **Eindruck**) subtly signals that the speaker isn't fully vouching for the impression being true,
as crystallized in the proverb-like **der Anschein trügt** ("appearances are deceiving"). The reflexive **sich
den Anschein geben** goes further, implying the person is actively performing/faking that impression.

## eifersüchtig

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** Jealous — feeling or showing resentful suspicion or envy toward a rival, or fear of losing
someone's affection or something one possesses, typically in romantic/relationship contexts but also usable
more broadly (e.g. envy of another's success or possessions).

**Synonym:** **neidisch** (more envy-of-possessions/success); **misstrauisch** (suspicious) in overlapping
contexts; opposite: **gelassen / vertrauensvoll**

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **eifersüchtig, eifersüchtige, eifersüchtiger, eifersüchtiges**.
Formed from **die Eifersucht** ("jealousy," itself from [[der Eifer]] + **die Sucht**, "addiction/compulsion")
+ **-ig**. Governs **auf (+ Akkusativ)**: **eifersüchtig auf jemanden/etwas sein** ("to be jealous of
someone/something"). Common constructions: **eifersüchtig werden** ("to become jealous"), **jemanden
eifersüchtig machen** ("to make someone jealous").

**Example:** *Er wurde eifersüchtig, als seine Freundin mit einem anderen tanzte.* — "He became jealous when
his girlfriend danced with someone else."

Another example: *Sie ist eifersüchtig auf den Erfolg ihrer Schwester.* — "She is jealous of her sister's
success."

**English:** **jealous** · **French:** **jaloux / jalouse**

Useful nuance: Note the spelling has an **s** before **üchtig**: **eifersüchtig**, not "eifersuchtig" — it's
built on the noun **die Eifersucht**, not directly on **der Eifer**. Unlike **neidisch**, which centers on
wanting what someone else has, **eifersüchtig** centers on the fear of losing someone's love/attention to a
rival, though the two senses can overlap in everyday usage.

## das Gewichtheben

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** Weightlifting — the sport of lifting heavy weights (barbells), either as competitive Olympic
weightlifting (snatch and clean & jerk) or more generally as a gym/strength-training activity.

**Synonym:** **das Krafttraining** (broader: strength training in general); **das Stemmen von Gewichten**
(descriptive paraphrase)

**Grammar:** Neuter noun: **das Gewichtheben**; uncountable, no plural (a nominalized infinitive). Compound of
**das Gewicht** ("weight") + **heben** ("to lift"). The agent noun is **der Gewichtheber / die
Gewichtheberin** ("weightlifter"). Common constructions: **Gewichtheben betreiben/trainieren** ("to practice/

Another example: *Gewichtheben stärkt nicht nur die Muskeln, sondern auch die Knochen.* — "Weightlifting
strengthens not only the muscles but also the bones."

**English:** **weightlifting** · **French:** **haltérophilie**

Useful nuance: **Gewichtheben** specifically evokes barbell lifting (often the Olympic lifts), whereas
**Krafttraining** is the broader umbrella term for any strength training, including machines, dumbbells, or

## vorausgesetzt

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Provided that / assuming that — used to introduce a condition that must hold for the main
statement to apply; can also function as a past participle meaning "presupposed/assumed."

**Synonym:** **unter der Voraussetzung, dass / wenn / sofern** depending on context

**Grammar:** Past participle of the verb **etwas (Akkusativ) voraussetzen** ("to presuppose, assume, require
as a precondition"), used as a subordinating conjunction/conditional marker. Common constructions:
**vorausgesetzt, dass...** ("provided that..."), and the shorter **vorausgesetzt + noun** (*vorausgesetzt gutes
Wetter*, "weather permitting"). Related noun: **die Voraussetzung** ("precondition, prerequisite").

**Example:** *Wir machen das Picknick, vorausgesetzt, dass es nicht regnet.* — "We'll have the picnic, provided
it doesn't rain."

Another example: *Vorausgesetzt gutes Wetter, findet das Konzert im Freien statt.* — "Weather permitting, the
concert takes place outdoors."

**English:** **provided that / assuming / given that** · **French:** **à condition que / pourvu que**

Useful nuance: **vorausgesetzt** is closely tied to **die Voraussetzung** ("precondition/prerequisite") — the
conjunction literally frames the following clause as a precondition that has been "set in advance"
(**voraus** + **setzen**). It reads as slightly more formal than **wenn** and is common in written German,
contracts, and careful spoken registers when spelling out a condition explicitly.

## beständig

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Constant, steady, or durable — describing something that persists reliably over time without
significant change: stable weather, a lasting relationship, a steady pace, or a material resistant to wear/
decay.

**Synonym:** **konstant / stabil / dauerhaft** depending on context; opposite: **unbeständig / wechselhaft**

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **beständig, beständige, beständiger, beständiges**. Related noun:
**die Beständigkeit** ("constancy, stability, durability"). Related verb: **bestehen** ("to exist, persist,
endure" — literally underlying "stand-through-ness"). Common constructions: **beständiges Wetter** ("stable
weather"), **beständig gegen etwas (Akkusativ)** ("resistant to something," e.g. *hitzebeständig*, "heat-
resistant"), and **beständig bleiben** ("to remain constant").

**Example:** *Seit Tagen haben wir beständiges, sonniges Wetter.* — "We've had stable, sunny weather for
days."

Another example: *Das Material ist sehr beständig gegen Hitze und Feuchtigkeit.* — "The material is very
resistant to heat and moisture."

**English:** **constant / steady / stable / durable / resistant** · **French:** **constant / stable /
durable**

Useful nuance: **beständig** appears both as a standalone adjective (steady weather, a steady pace) and as the
second half of many compound "resistant to X" adjectives (**hitzebeständig**, **wetterbeständig**,
**korrosionsbeständig**). In that compound role it functions much like English "-proof" or "-resistant." The
noun **Beständigkeit** is the more abstract counterpart to [[die Mäßigung]]-type virtue words — steadfastness
or consistency of character, as opposed to moderation.

## der Antrieb

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Drive or motivation — the inner impulse or incentive that moves someone to act; also, in a
technical/mechanical sense, the propulsion system or drive mechanism of a vehicle or machine (engine,
transmission, motor).

**Synonym:** **die Motivation / der Ansporn / der Impuls** (motivation sense); **der Motor / das Triebwerk**
(mechanical sense)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Antrieb**; plural: **die Antriebe**. Derived from the separable verb
**jemanden/etwas antreiben** ("to drive/propel someone or something forward, to spur on"). Common
constructions: **aus eigenem Antrieb** ("on one's own initiative/of one's own accord"), **jemandem Antrieb
geben** ("to give someone motivation/drive"), and, mechanically, **elektrischer Antrieb** ("electric drive/
powertrain"), **Antrieb auf alle vier Räder** ("all-wheel drive").

**Example:** *Ihr größter Antrieb war der Wunsch, ihrer Familie zu helfen.* — "Her greatest motivation was the
desire to help her family."

Another example: *Das neue Auto hat einen elektrischen Antrieb.* — "The new car has an electric drive/
powertrain."

**English:** **drive / motivation / impetus; propulsion / drivetrain (mechanical)** · **French:** **motivation
/ élan; propulsion / motorisation**

Useful nuance: The idiom **aus eigenem Antrieb** ("on one's own initiative," literally "from one's own drive")
captures the word's core sense well — an internally generated impulse to act, without external prompting. This
sits near [[der Eifer]] (zeal/enthusiasm) but **Antrieb** is more neutral and mechanical-sounding, describing
the underlying motivational "engine" itself rather than the enthusiastic energy of pursuing something.

## begrüßenswert

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** Welcome, commendable, or to be welcomed — describing a development, decision, or initiative
that is viewed positively and deserves approval or endorsement, often in formal, journalistic, or official
contexts.

**Synonym:** **erfreulich / löblich / positiv zu bewerten** depending on context; opposite: **bedauerlich /
unerwünscht**

**Grammar:** Adjective, regular declension: **begrüßenswert, begrüßenswerte, begrüßenswerter,
begrüßenswertes**. Formed from **begrüßen** ("to welcome, greet, approve of") + **-wert** ("worthy of," as in
**lobenswert**, "praiseworthy"; **lesenswert**, "worth reading"). Common constructions: **eine begrüßenswerte
Initiative** ("a welcome initiative"), **es ist begrüßenswert, dass...** ("it is to be welcomed that...").

Another example: *Es ist begrüßenswert, dass sich immer mehr Unternehmen für Nachhaltigkeit einsetzen.* — "It
is welcome that more and more companies are committing themselves to sustainability."

**English:** **welcome / commendable / laudable** · **French:** **louable / bienvenu(e)**

Useful nuance: **begrüßenswert** belongs to a family of **-wert** adjectives (**lobenswert, lesenswert,
empfehlenswert**) that all mean "worthy of X." It has a notably formal, written register — common in news
commentary, official statements, and reports — and is rarely used in casual speech, where **gut/toll/schön**
would be more natural.

## derartig / Derartiges

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** Such, of such a kind, or something of that sort — a formal way of referring back to something
previously mentioned or implied without repeating it, used both as an attributive adjective ("derartige
Probleme," "such problems") and, capitalized, as a nominalized pronoun ("Derartiges," "something of that
kind/anything like that").

**Synonym:** **solch- / so etwas / dergleichen** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective **derartig, derartige, derartiger, derartiges** (declines like a normal adjective) built
from **der Art** ("of the/that kind") + **-ig**. When nominalized and capitalized, **Derartiges** functions
like an indefinite pronoun (similar to **etwas Ähnliches**, "something similar") and is typically used after
negation or in questions: **nichts Derartiges** ("nothing of the kind"), **so etwas wie Derartiges** (rare,
usually just **so etwas**). Common constructions: **derartige Vorfälle** ("such incidents"), **habe ich noch
nie erlebt** paired with **Derartiges** ("I have never experienced anything like that").

**Example:** *Derartige Vorfälle dürfen sich nicht wiederholen.* — "Such incidents must not happen again."

Another example: *Ich habe noch nie Derartiges gesehen.* — "I have never seen anything like that."

**English:** **such / of that kind; something like that / anything of the sort** · **French:** **tel/telle;
quelque chose de ce genre**

Useful nuance: **derartig** and its nominalized form **Derartiges** are formal register, common in news
reports, official statements, and legal/administrative language, where **so etwas** or **so ein(e)** would be
the everyday spoken equivalent. **Derartiges** almost always appears with a negation (**nichts Derartiges**)
or in a context implying "nothing/anything comparable," much like English "anything of the sort."

## zurückgreifen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To fall back on, resort to, or draw upon — to make use of something (a resource, method,
strategy, past experience, or reserve) when needed, typically because it is available, familiar, or necessary
as an alternative or backup.

**Synonym:** **zurückkommen auf / sich bedienen (+ Genitiv) / in Anspruch nehmen** depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable verb: **zurückgreifen, griff zurück, hat zurückgegriffen**. Governs **auf (+
Akkusativ)**: **auf etwas (Akkusativ) zurückgreifen** ("to fall back on/resort to something"). Related to
**greifen** ("to grasp/reach") + **zurück** ("back"), literally "to reach back [for something]." Common
constructions: **auf alte Methoden zurückgreifen** ("to fall back on old methods"), **auf Ersparnisse
zurückgreifen** ("to draw on savings"), **auf Erfahrung zurückgreifen** ("to draw on experience").

**Example:** *In der Krise musste das Unternehmen auf seine Rücklagen zurückgreifen.* — "In the crisis, the
company had to draw on its reserves."

Another example: *Sie griff bei der Reparatur auf ihre jahrelange Erfahrung zurück.* — "She drew on her years
of experience during the repair."

**English:** **to fall back on / resort to / draw upon** · **French:** **recourir à / avoir recours à**

Useful nuance: **zurückgreifen** carries a neutral-to-positive tone — using an available resource sensibly —
unlike **sich mit etwas begnügen** or negatively-tinged "resort to" phrasings in English. It's very common in
formal/written German for describing institutions or people making use of existing methods, funds, or
knowledge rather than inventing something new.

## der Spieß

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A skewer, spit, or pike/lance — a long pointed rod used for roasting food (skewer/spit) or,
historically, as a military weapon (pike/lance); colloquially, also a barracks slang term for a company
sergeant major.

**Synonym:** **der Grillspieß** (skewer specifically); **die Lanze / die Pike** (weapon sense)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **der Spieß**; plural: **die Spieße**. Related verb: **etwas aufspießen** ("to
skewer/impale something"). Common idiom: **den Spieß umdrehen** ("to turn the tables," literally "to turn the
spit/pike around" — i.e. to reverse a situation and use an opponent's own tactic against them). Also:
**gespickt wie am Spieß** (colloquial, "stuffed/crammed full").

**Example:** *Er hat das Fleisch für den Grill auf einen Spieß gesteckt.* — "He put the meat on a skewer for
the grill."

Another example: *Nachdem sie ständig kritisiert wurde, drehte sie den Spieß um und stellte ihm unbequeme
Fragen.* — "After being constantly criticized, she turned the tables and asked him uncomfortable questions."

**English:** **skewer / spit; pike/lance (weapon)** · **French:** **brochette / broche; pique/lance**

Useful nuance: The idiom **den Spieß umdrehen** is by far the most common modern use of **Spieß** in everyday
speech — it vividly evokes flipping a weapon around to point it back at whoever was wielding it, and is used
whenever someone reverses an attack, accusation, or disadvantageous situation back onto its originator.

## das Hintertreffen

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** A position of disadvantage or being behind — used almost exclusively in the fixed phrase **ins
Hintertreffen geraten/kommen** ("to fall behind, end up at a disadvantage") and its counterpart **im
Hintertreffen sein** ("to be at a disadvantage/behind").

**Synonym:** **der Nachteil / die Benachteiligung** (as a description of the resulting state)

**Grammar:** Neuter noun: **das Hintertreffen**; uncountable, essentially only used in the fixed phrases **ins
Hintertreffen geraten/kommen** and **im Hintertreffen sein/liegen**. Historically derived from card-game/
military vocabulary describing being pushed to the rear or losing ground. Rarely used outside these set
expressions.

**Example:** *Ohne Investitionen in neue Technik gerät die Firma schnell ins Hintertreffen.* — "Without
investment in new technology, the company quickly falls behind."

Another example: *Im internationalen Vergleich liegt Deutschland bei der Digitalisierung im Hintertreffen.* —
"By international comparison, Germany is behind when it comes to digitalization."

**English:** **a position of disadvantage / falling behind (in set phrases)** · **French:** **être désavantagé
/ prendre du retard**

Useful nuance: **Hintertreffen** essentially only survives today inside the fixed idiom **ins Hintertreffen
geraten/kommen**; it isn't used as a free-standing noun elsewhere in modern German. Related to [[geraten]],
which supplies the "falling into" sense of ending up in that disadvantaged position.

## geraten

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To end up, fall into, or come to be in a certain (usually unplanned or undesirable) state or
situation — used with **in/an/unter + Akkusativ** to describe drifting or unintentionally arriving at a
condition, place, or circumstance. As a separate adjective (from a different verb, **raten**), **geraten**
also means "advisable/wise" (e.g. **es wäre geraten...**, "it would be advisable...").

**Synonym:** **hineingeraten / hineinrutschen / verfallen (in)** depending on context

**Grammar:** Strong verb: **geraten, geriet, ist geraten** (conjugates with **sein**). Governs prepositional
phrases: **in Schwierigkeiten geraten** ("to get into difficulties"), **in Vergessenheit geraten** ("to fall
into oblivion"), **außer Kontrolle geraten** ("to get out of control"), **an jemanden geraten** ("to run into/
end up dealing with someone," often a difficult person), **unter Verdacht geraten** ("to come under
suspicion"). Related noun: **das Hintertreffen** in the phrase **ins Hintertreffen geraten**.

**Example:** *Nach dem Unfall geriet er in Panik.* — "After the accident, he fell into a panic."

Another example: *Die alte Tradition ist mittlerweile fast in Vergessenheit geraten.* — "The old tradition has
by now almost fallen into oblivion."

**English:** **to end up / fall into / come to be (in a state); (separately, as adjective) advisable** ·
**French:** **tomber dans / se retrouver (dans un état)**

Useful nuance: **geraten** describes arriving at a state somewhat passively or unintentionally — you don't
deliberately "geraten in Schwierigkeiten," it happens to you — which is why it pairs so naturally with
negative or uncontrolled situations (panic, oblivion, suspicion, disadvantage as in [[das Hintertreffen]]).
This is distinct from the homonymous adjective **geraten** ("advisable"), which comes from the unrelated verb
**raten** ("to advise/guess").

## einschränken

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To restrict, limit, or curtail — to reduce the scope, extent, or freedom of something (rights,
spending, options, a statement) or, reflexively, to cut back on one's own consumption/spending/lifestyle.

**Synonym:** **begrenzen / beschränken / reduzieren** depending on context; opposite: **ausweiten / erweitern**

**Grammar:** Separable verb: **einschränken, schränkte ein, hat eingeschränkt**. Transitive **etwas
(Akkusativ) einschränken** ("to restrict/limit something") and reflexive **sich (Akkusativ) einschränken**
("to cut back, economize, limit oneself"). Related noun: **die Einschränkung** ("restriction, limitation,
qualification"). Common constructions: **die Rechte einschränken** ("to restrict rights"), **den Konsum
einschränken** ("to cut back consumption"), **ohne Einschränkung** ("without restriction/qualification, i.e.
unreservedly").

**Example:** *Wegen der Pandemie mussten viele Geschäfte ihre Öffnungszeiten einschränken.* — "Because of the
pandemic, many shops had to restrict their opening hours."

Another example: *Nach dem Jobverlust musste sich die Familie finanziell stark einschränken.* — "After losing
the job, the family had to cut back a lot financially."

**English:** **to restrict / limit / curtail; (reflexive) to cut back/economize** · **French:** **restreindre
/ limiter**

Useful nuance: **einschränken** is closely related to [[maßvoll]] and [[die Mäßigung]] in spirit — where
**Mäßigung** names the general virtue of restraint, **einschränken** is the concrete verb for actively reducing
something specific (rights, spending, a claim). It also appears often as a qualifying phrase in argumentation,
e.g. **das muss man einschränken** ("that needs to be qualified/that's not entirely true"), signaling a
caveat to a previous statement.

## überlassen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To leave (something) to someone, hand over, or entrust — to let another person take
responsibility for, decide on, or have something, rather than dealing with it oneself; also used reflexively/
passively to mean "to be left to" a fate, chance, or one's own devices.

**Synonym:** **übergeben / abtreten / anvertrauen** depending on context

**Grammar:** Inseparable strong verb: **überlassen, überließ, hat überlassen**. Governs dative of person +
accusative of thing: **jemandem etwas (Akkusativ) überlassen** ("to leave/hand something over to someone").
Common constructions: **jemandem die Entscheidung überlassen** ("to leave the decision to someone"), **nichts
dem Zufall überlassen** ("to leave nothing to chance"), **sich (Dativ) selbst überlassen sein** ("to be left to
one's own devices").

**Example:** *Ich überlasse dir die Wahl des Restaurants.* — "I'll leave the choice of restaurant to you."

Another example: *Ein guter Pilot überlässt nichts dem Zufall.* — "A good pilot leaves nothing to chance."

**English:** **to leave to / hand over / entrust; (passive) to be left to** · **French:** **laisser (à) /
confier / abandonner (à)**

Useful nuance: The idiom **nichts dem Zufall überlassen** ("to leave nothing to chance") is extremely common
and captures the verb's core sense well: actively choosing not to intervene or control something, ceding that
control to another person, to fate, or to circumstance. **sich selbst überlassen sein** carries a notably
bleaker connotation — being abandoned or left without support/guidance.

## überstürzt

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** Hasty, rash, or precipitate — describing an action, decision, or departure carried out too
quickly, without sufficient thought, preparation, or care, often with a negative connotation of recklessness.

**Synonym:** **voreilig / übereilt / unüberlegt** depending on context; opposite: **besonnen / wohlüberlegt**

**Grammar:** Adjective/past participle of the reflexive verb **sich überstürzen** ("to happen too fast, to be
rash, to fall over oneself [in haste]" — literally "to over-topple/over-tumble"); also transitive **etwas
überstürzen** ("to rush something"). Declines like a normal adjective: **überstürzt, überstürzte,
überstürzter, überstürztes** (as in the inflected form **überstürzten**, e.g. **überstürzten Entscheidungen**,
"hasty decisions"). Common constructions: **eine überstürzte Entscheidung** ("a rash/hasty decision"), **nichts
überstürzen** ("not to rush anything"), **überstürzt handeln** ("to act hastily").

**Example:** *Er bereute später seine überstürzte Entscheidung, den Job zu kündigen.* — "He later regretted his
hasty decision to quit the job."

Another example: *Man sollte bei so wichtigen Fragen nichts überstürzen.* — "One shouldn't rush anything on
such important questions."

**English:** **hasty / rash / precipitate** · **French:** **précipité / hâtif**

Useful nuance: **überstürzt** is closely related to [[maßvoll]] and [[die Mäßigung]] as its opposite pole — where
those words praise measured restraint, **überstürzt** criticizes acting too fast without due consideration. The
common advice phrase **nichts überstürzen** ("don't rush things") is a very natural, everyday way to counsel
patience before a decision.

## zufügen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To inflict or cause (something harmful) — to bring about pain, damage, injury, or loss for
someone, typically used with abstract negative nouns like **Schaden, Schmerz, Leid, Unrecht**. The "zu +
infinitive" form **zuzufügen** ("to inflict") appears after modal-like constructions (e.g. **um... zuzufügen**,
"in order to inflict...").

**Synonym:** **verursachen / antun / bereiten** depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable verb: **zufügen, fügte zu, hat zugefügt**. Governs dative of person + accusative of
harm: **jemandem etwas (Akkusativ) zufügen** ("to inflict something on someone"). Common constructions:
**jemandem Schaden zufügen** ("to cause someone damage/harm"), **sich (Dativ) selbst Schaden zufügen** ("to
harm oneself"), **jemandem Schmerzen/Leid zufügen** ("to inflict pain/suffering on someone").

**Example:** *Der Sturm hat der Region erheblichen Schaden zugefügt.* — "The storm inflicted considerable
damage on the region."

Another example: *Er wollte ihr mit seinen Worten keinen Schmerz zufügen.* — "He didn't want to inflict any
pain on her with his words."

**English:** **to inflict / cause (harm)** · **French:** **infliger / causer**

Useful nuance: **zufügen** almost always pairs with a negative abstract noun as its object (**Schaden, Leid,
Schmerz, Unrecht**) — it's rarely used for inflicting something positive. Compare **jemandem etwas antun**,
which is more personal/emotional in register (often used for betrayal or cruelty toward a specific person),
whereas **zufügen** is more neutral/formal and works equally well for large-scale or impersonal harm (storm
damage, economic damage).

## stürzen

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** To fall (heavily/suddenly), plunge, or crash — describing a sudden, often uncontrolled fall or
drop (a person tripping and falling, a plane crashing, prices/values plummeting); also transitively/
figuratively, to overthrow or topple (a government, a ruler), and reflexively, to rush or throw oneself into
something.

**Synonym:** **fallen** (more general/neutral); **abstürzen** (crash, especially of planes/prices/computers);
**umstürzen** (topple over); **stürmen** (rush, storm) for the reflexive sense

**Grammar:** Strong verb: **stürzen, stürzte, ist/hat gestürzt** — intransitive with **sein** (**er ist
gestürzt**, "he fell/took a tumble"), transitive with **haben** (**er hat die Regierung gestürzt**, "he
toppled the government"). Reflexive: **sich (Akkusativ) in/auf etwas (Akkusativ) stürzen** ("to throw oneself
into/at something"). Common constructions: **die Treppe hinunterstürzen** ("to fall down the stairs"), **die
Preise/Aktien stürzen** ("prices/stocks plunge"), **einen Diktator stürzen** ("to overthrow a dictator"), **sich
in die Arbeit stürzen** ("to throw oneself into work").

**Example:** *Er ist auf der vereisten Straße gestürzt und hat sich das Bein gebrochen.* — "He fell on the icy
street and broke his leg."

Another example: *Nach dem Skandal stürzte die Regierung innerhalb weniger Tage.* — "After the scandal, the
government fell/collapsed within a few days."

**English:** **to fall/plunge/crash; to overthrow/topple (transitive); to throw oneself into (reflexive)** ·
**French:** **tomber/chuter; renverser (transitif); se jeter (réfléchi)**

Useful nuance: **stürzen** carries more force and suddenness than plain **fallen** — it's the word for a heavy,
dramatic fall (down stairs, off a horse) or a rapid, uncontrolled drop (stock prices, a government), not a
gentle "falling." The reflexive **sich in etwas stürzen** extends this dramatic intensity metaphorically to
eagerly throwing oneself into an activity, echoing the enthusiastic energy of [[der Eifer]].

## verwandeln

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** To transform or turn (something/someone) into something else — to change the nature, form, or
character of a thing or person, often completely; reflexively, **sich in etwas (Akkusativ) verwandeln** means
"to turn into/become something" (a change undergone by the subject itself). The present participle
**verwandelnd** ("transforming") appears in constructions like **etwas in etwas verwandelnd** ("transforming
something into something").

**Synonym:** **umwandeln / verändern / umformen** depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak, inseparable verb: **verwandeln, verwandelte, hat verwandelt**. Transitive **etwas
(Akkusativ) in etwas (Akkusativ) verwandeln** ("to transform something into something") and reflexive **sich
in etwas (Akkusativ) verwandeln** ("to turn/transform into something"). Related noun: **die Verwandlung**
("transformation, metamorphosis"). Common constructions: **Wasser in Wein verwandeln** ("to turn water into
wine"), **sich in einen Frosch verwandeln** ("to turn into a frog").

**Example:** *Die Kälte verwandelte den See innerhalb einer Nacht in eine Eisfläche.* — "The cold transformed
the lake into a sheet of ice within a single night."

Another example: *In der Geschichte verwandelt sich der Prinz in einen Frosch.* — "In the story, the prince
turns into a frog."

**English:** **to transform / turn into / change (into)** · **French:** **transformer / (se) changer (en)**

Useful nuance: **verwandeln** (related to **die Verwandlung**, famously the title of Kafka's *Die Verwandlung*,
"The Metamorphosis") implies a thorough, often dramatic change in essential nature or form, closer to English
"transform/metamorphose" than the more neutral **verändern** ("to change/alter"), which can describe minor or
gradual change.

## auffallen

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** To stand out, be noticeable, or catch one's attention — to strike someone as conspicuous,
unusual, or worth noting, whether visually (bright colors, an odd appearance) or through behavior/qualities
(a mistake, a talent, an unusual habit).

**Synonym:** **sich abheben / ins Auge fallen / bemerkbar sein** depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable strong verb: **auffallen, fiel auf, ist aufgefallen** (conjugates with **sein**).
Governs dative of the observer: **jemandem auffallen** ("to catch someone's attention, be noticed by
someone"), e.g. **mir ist aufgefallen, dass...** ("I noticed that..."). Related adjective: **auffallend /
auffällig** ("noticeable, conspicuous, striking"). Common constructions: **positiv/negativ auffallen** ("to
stand out favorably/unfavorably"), **jemandem fällt etwas auf** ("someone notices something").

**Example:** *Mir ist aufgefallen, dass du in letzter Zeit sehr müde wirkst.* — "I've noticed that you've
seemed very tired lately."

Another example: *Sie fiel schon als Kind durch ihr außergewöhnliches musikalisches Talent auf.* — "Even as a
child, she stood out for her extraordinary musical talent."

**English:** **to stand out / be noticeable / catch one's attention / notice** · **French:** **se faire
remarquer / remarquer**

Useful nuance: The impersonal construction **jemandem fällt etwas auf** (literally "something falls onto
someone['s attention]") is the standard way to say "I/you/someone noticed something" — grammatically the
thing noticed is the subject, and the observer takes the dative, which is a common trap for English speakers
who instinctively want to make the observer the subject (as in "I noticed").

## scheuen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To shy away from, avoid, or be averse to something — typically out of fear, discomfort, or
reluctance; used transitively for effort/risk/cost ("to shun, not shrink from") and reflexively with **vor +
dative** for fears or reluctance about doing something. Also used specifically of animals, especially horses,
to mean "to shy, bolt, startle."

**Synonym:** **meiden / vermeiden** (avoid) · **sich fürchten vor** (be afraid of) · **zurückschrecken vor**
(shrink back from)

**Grammar:** Weak verb: **scheuen, scheute, hat gescheut**. Transitive use: **etwas scheuen** ("to shun/not
shrink from something"), especially in the fixed idiom **keine Mühe/Kosten scheuen** ("to spare no
effort/expense," literally "to shy away from no effort/costs"). Reflexive use: **sich vor etwas scheuen**
("to be averse to / shrink from something"), often followed by an infinitive clause: **sich scheuen, etwas
zu tun** ("to be afraid/reluctant to do something"). Intransitive use for animals: **das Pferd scheut** ("the
horse shies/bolts").

**Example:** *Er scheute sich nicht, seine Meinung offen zu sagen.* — "He wasn't afraid to say his opinion
openly."

Another example: *Das Pferd scheute plötzlich vor dem lauten Geräusch.* — "The horse suddenly shied at the
loud noise."

**English:** **to shy away from / avoid / be averse to / (of animals) to shy, bolt** · **French:** **éviter /
craindre / (cheval) s'effaroucher**

Useful nuance: The idiom **keine Mühe/Kosten scheuen** ("to spare no effort/expense") is very common in
formal or business German and literally reverses the English phrasing — instead of saying what *is* spent, it
says what is *not* avoided. Don't confuse **scheuen** with the reflexive **sich schämen** ("to be ashamed"):
**scheuen** is about reluctance/fear of an action, not embarrassment about oneself.

## ergreifen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To seize, grasp, or take hold of something — literally (an object, someone's hand) or, much
more commonly, figuratively (a measure, an opportunity, power, a profession, the floor in a discussion).
Separately, the past participle **ergriffen** functions as an adjective meaning "moved, touched, deeply
affected" emotionally.

**Synonym:** **packen / fassen** (physical grasp) · **nutzen / wahrnehmen** (an opportunity) · **an sich
reißen** (power, more forceful)

**Grammar:** Strong, inseparable verb: **ergreifen, ergriff, hat ergriffen**. Governs the accusative: **etwas
(Akkusativ) ergreifen**. Common fixed constructions: **Maßnahmen ergreifen** ("to take measures"), **die
Gelegenheit/Chance ergreifen** ("to seize the opportunity"), **die Macht ergreifen** ("to seize power"),
**einen Beruf ergreifen** ("to take up a profession"), **die Flucht ergreifen** ("to flee," literally "to seize
flight"), **das Wort ergreifen** ("to take the floor, start speaking"). The past participle **ergriffen** is
also used as a standalone adjective: **von etwas ergriffen sein/werden** ("to be moved/overcome by
something").

**Example:** *Die Regierung musste schnell Maßnahmen ergreifen, um die Ausbreitung der Krankheit zu stoppen.*
— "The government had to quickly take measures to stop the spread of the disease."

Another example: *Nach der bewegenden Rede war das gesamte Publikum sichtlich ergriffen.* — "After the moving
speech, the entire audience was visibly touched."

**English:** **to seize / grasp / take hold of; to take (measures/action); (as adjective, ergriffen) moved /
touched** · **French:** **saisir / prendre (des mesures); (ergriffen) ému(e)**

Useful nuance: **ergreifen** adds the prefix **er-** (marking a completive, successful outcome) to the plain
verb **greifen** ("to grasp/reach"), and extends easily from literal grasping to abstract "seizing" of
opportunities, power, or a profession — much like English "seize" covers both a seized object and a seized
chance. The adjectival **ergriffen** is the standard word for describing an audience or individual visibly
moved by a speech, piece of music, or ceremony, distinct from **gerührt** (touched, more intimate/personal,
e.g. by a kind gesture) which leans gentler and more personal.

## der Narr

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A fool or jester — someone acting foolishly or unwisely, or, historically, a court entertainer
whose role was to jest, mock, and speak freely; also the central figure of German carnival (Karneval/Fastnacht)
tradition, where costumed revelers are called **Narren**.

**Synonym:** **der Hofnarr** (court jester specifically) · **der Dummkopf** (fool/idiot, more insulting) ·
**der Clown**

**Grammar:** Masculine weak noun: **der Narr, des Narren, dem Narren, den Narren** — takes the weak **-en**
ending in genitive, dative, and accusative singular (unlike a regular masculine noun); plural: **die Narren**.
Related verb: **jemanden narren** ("to fool/dupe someone," somewhat literary/dated). Common idioms: **jemanden
zum Narren halten** ("to make a fool of someone, to dupe/trick someone"), **Narrenfreiheit** ("fool's license,"
the freedom to say or do as one pleases without consequence).

**Example:** *Am Rosenmontag ziehen Tausende von Narren in bunten Kostümen durch die Straßen.* — "On Rosenmontag
[carnival Monday], thousands of costumed fools/revelers parade through the streets."

Another example: *Er fühlte sich zum Narren gehalten, als er die Wahrheit erfuhr.* — "He felt made a fool of
when he learned the truth."

**English:** **fool / jester / clown** · **French:** **fou / bouffon**

Useful nuance: Note the weak noun declension — **der Narr** takes **-en** in the genitive, dative, and
accusative singular (**des/dem/den Narren**), a common source of error since it looks like a regular masculine
noun in the nominative. **Narrenfreiheit** captures the traditional social license granted to jesters and, by
extension, to carnival revelers or outspoken individuals today, to mock authority or speak bluntly without
facing the usual consequences.

## immer gleich

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Right away, immediately, or straightaway — an intensified adverbial phrase combining **immer**
("always") with **gleich** in its temporal sense ("right away, at once"), typically used to emphasize that
something happens instantly, every single time, often with a critical or impatient undertone (as in
complaining that a reaction is too quick or reflexive).

**Synonym:** **sofort / auf der Stelle / augenblicklich** (immediately, without the "always" component) ·
**gleich** alone (right away, in a moment)

**Grammar:** Adverbial combination of **immer** ("always") + **gleich** used as a temporal adverb ("right
away, at once, in a moment," as in *Ich komme gleich*, "I'm coming right away") — not the homonymous adjective
**gleich** meaning "same, equal" (*das gleiche Buch*, "the same book"). No inflection; used directly before or
after the verb it modifies. Common pattern: **immer gleich + adjective/verb** (*immer gleich beleidigt sein*,
"to always get offended right away"; *immer gleich Partei ergreifen*, "to always immediately take sides").

**Example:** *Warum musst du immer gleich beleidigt sein?* — "Why do you always have to get offended right
away?"

Another example: *Nur Narren ergreifen immer gleich Partei.* — "Only fools always immediately take sides."

**English:** **right away / immediately / straightaway (every time)** · **French:** **tout de suite /
immédiatement (à chaque fois)**

Useful nuance: **gleich** alone already carries the temporal meaning "in a moment/right away" (distinct from
its adjectival sense "same/equal"); adding **immer** turns a single instance of promptness into a criticized
pattern — someone who reacts instantly and predictably every time, as in [[der Narr]]-type impulsiveness
(jumping to take sides, getting offended) rather than showing restraint.

## Partei ergreifen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To take sides — to declare or show support for one side of a dispute, argument, or conflict,
rather than remaining neutral.

**Synonym:** **Partei nehmen / Stellung beziehen (für)** depending on context; opposite: **neutral bleiben /
sich heraushalten**

**Grammar:** Fixed idiom combining [[ergreifen]] ("to seize/take") with **die Partei** used in its older,
broader sense of "side, party to a dispute" (not "political party"). Governs **für**: **für jemanden/etwas
Partei ergreifen** ("to take someone's/something's side"). Conjugates as **ergreifen**: *er ergreift Partei,
er ergriff Partei, er hat Partei ergriffen*.

**Example:** *In dem Streit wollte sie nicht für eine Seite Partei ergreifen.* — "In the dispute, she didn't
want to take sides for either party."

Another example: *Nur Narren ergreifen immer gleich Partei.* — "Only fools always immediately take sides."

**English:** **to take sides / side with someone** · **French:** **prendre parti / prendre position**

Useful nuance: **Partei** in this idiom keeps its older, broader meaning of "side/party to a dispute" —
similar to English "party" in legal contexts ("a party to the lawsuit") — rather than "political party." The
phrase is neutral in register and works equally for personal quarrels, debates, and formal/legal contexts;
adding **für jemanden** specifies exactly whose side is taken.

## indem

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** By (doing something) — a subordinating conjunction introducing a clause that states the means
or method by which the action in the main clause is achieved, equivalent to English "by + -ing."

**Synonym:** **dadurch, dass...** (more explicit paraphrase, "through the fact that...")

**Grammar:** Subordinating conjunction — sends the finite verb to the end of its own clause: *Er half ihr,
indem er die Kinder betreute.* The **indem**-clause can also come first, in which case it occupies the first
position and the main clause verb follows in second position: *Indem er die Kinder betreute, half er ihr.*
Not to be confused with **während** ("while," expressing simultaneity or contrast, not means) or the archaic
adverb **indessen** ("meanwhile / however").

**Example:** *Sie verbesserte ihre Aussprache, indem sie jeden Tag laut las.* — "She improved her
pronunciation by reading aloud every day."

Another example: *Indem er früh aufstand, schaffte er es, immer pünktlich zur Arbeit zu kommen.* — "By getting
up early, he managed to always get to work on time."

**English:** **by (doing something)** · **French:** **en (+ gérondif)**, e.g. *en lisant* ("by reading")

Useful nuance: **indem** specifically marks the *means or method* by which something is accomplished — the
"how." English speakers often reach for **während** instead, since English "while" can loosely cover both
simultaneity and method ("while reading, she..."), but German keeps the two apart: **während** signals two
things happening at the same time (or a contrast), while **indem** signals that the subordinate clause's
action *is the mechanism* that brings about the main clause's result.

## erhören

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** To hear and grant, answer favorably, or heed — used especially of a prayer, wish, or plea that
is heard and fulfilled (by God, fate, or someone with the power to grant it); in older, literary/romantic
usage, said of a woman who "hears" and reciprocates a suitor's declaration of love or courtship.

**Synonym:** **erfüllen** (to fulfill, more general) · **stattgeben (+ Dativ)** (to grant a request, more
formal/legal register)

**Grammar:** Weak, inseparable verb: **erhören, erhörte, hat erhört**. Governs the accusative: **ein Gebet /
eine Bitte / einen Wunsch erhören** ("to hear and grant a prayer/request/wish"), or, in the romantic sense,
**jemandes Liebe/Werben erhören** ("to accept/reciprocate someone's love/courtship"). Very common in the
passive: **sein Gebet wurde erhört** ("his prayer was answered/heard").

**Example:** *Endlich wurden ihre Gebete erhört, und der ersehnte Regen setzte ein.* — "Finally her prayers
were answered, and the longed-for rain began."

Another example: *In dem alten Roman erhört die Prinzessin schließlich das Werben des jungen Ritters.* — "In
the old novel, the princess finally accepts the young knight's courtship."

**English:** **to hear and grant / answer (a prayer/wish); (literary) to accept a suitor's love** ·
**French:** **exaucer (une prière, un vœu); (littéraire) accepter les avances de qn**

Useful nuance: **erhören** carries a formal, often religious or literary register — close to French
**exaucer** for prayers and wishes. It's stronger than plain **hören** ("to hear"): **erhören** implies not
just perceiving a request but responding to it favorably and granting it, and the idiom **sein Gebet/seine
Bitte wurde erhört** is the standard, slightly elevated way to say a prayer or wish "was answered."

## die Weigerung

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Refusal — the act of declining or refusing to do something, or a statement expressing that
refusal.

**Synonym:** **die Ablehnung** (rejection, broader) · **die Verweigerung** (refusal in a more formal/
institutional sense, e.g. **die Kriegsdienstverweigerung**, "conscientious objection")

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Weigerung**; plural: **die Weigerungen**. Derived from the reflexive verb
**sich weigern, etwas zu tun** ("to refuse to do something"). Common constructions: **seine Weigerung, etwas
zu tun** ("his refusal to do something"), **trotz seiner Weigerung** ("despite his refusal").

**Example:** *Seine Weigerung, die Unterlagen vorzulegen, sorgte für großes Misstrauen.* — "His refusal to
submit the documents caused great suspicion."

Another example: *Trotz mehrfacher Aufforderung blieb sie bei ihrer Weigerung.* — "Despite repeated requests,
she stuck to her refusal."

**English:** **refusal** · **French:** **refus**

Useful nuance: **die Weigerung** is the everyday, personal word for someone's simple refusal to do something,
tied directly to the reflexive verb **sich weigern**. **die Verweigerung** sounds more formal or institutional
and often appears in fixed legal/administrative compounds (**Aussageverweigerung**, "refusal to testify";
**Kriegsdienstverweigerung**, "conscientious objection") rather than describing an individual's everyday
refusal.

## sich weigern

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** To refuse — to decline or resist doing something that is asked, expected, or required of one,
often implying active resistance, defiance, or stubbornness rather than a neutral decline.

**Synonym:** **sich widersetzen** (to oppose/resist, stronger) · **ablehnen** (to decline/reject an offer or
proposal — a related but distinct meaning, see nuance below)

**Grammar:** Weak reflexive verb: **sich weigern, weigerte sich, hat sich geweigert**. Standard construction:
**sich weigern, etwas zu tun** ("to refuse to do something"). Related noun: [[die Weigerung]] ("refusal").

**Example:** *Er weigerte sich, den Vertrag zu unterschreiben.* — "He refused to sign the contract."

Another example: *Die Arbeiter weigerten sich, unter diesen Bedingungen weiterzuarbeiten.* — "The workers
refused to keep working under these conditions."

**English:** **to refuse** · **French:** **refuser (de faire qc) / se refuser à**

Useful nuance: **sich weigern** is about actively refusing to *perform* an action asked or expected of you —
often with a tone of defiance or stubborn resistance (signing a document, following an order, continuing
work) — whereas **ablehnen** is the more neutral verb for declining something *offered* to you (a request, an
invitation, an application). Compare *er lehnt das Angebot ab* ("he declines the offer," neutral) with *er
weigert sich, das zu unterschreiben* ("he refuses to sign it," resistant/defiant).

## beschwören

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** To swear (an oath) solemnly, to conjure or invoke (spirits, magic), to implore or beseech
someone earnestly and urgently, or to evoke/conjure up (a memory, image, feeling).

**Synonym:** **schwören** (swear, more neutral) · **anflehen** (implore/beg, less solemn) · **herbeirufen /
zitieren** (conjure/invoke, magical sense) · **heraufbeschwören** (closely related separable compound: to
conjure up/bring about, often something negative)

**Grammar:** Strong, inseparable verb: **beschwören, beschwor, hat beschworen**. Governs accusative: **einen
Eid beschwören** ("to swear an oath"), **einen Geist beschwören** ("to conjure a spirit"), **jemanden
beschwören, etwas [nicht] zu tun** ("to implore someone to [not] do something"), **Erinnerungen beschwören**
("to evoke memories"). Related noun: **die Beschwörung** ("incantation, conjuring, adjuration"). Related
separable verb **heraufbeschwören** specifically means "to conjure up/summon into being" and is often used for
unintentionally causing trouble: **ein Unglück heraufbeschwören** ("to bring disaster upon oneself/court
disaster").

**Example:** *Er beschwor seinen Freund, die gefährliche Reise nicht anzutreten.* — "He implored his friend
not to embark on the dangerous journey."

Another example: *Der Zeuge musste vor Gericht beschwören, die Wahrheit gesagt zu haben.* — "The witness had to
swear before the court that he had told the truth."

**English:** **to swear (an oath) / to conjure, invoke / to implore, beseech / to evoke** · **French:** **jurer
(un serment) / invoquer, conjurer / supplier / évoquer**

Useful nuance: The "implore" sense of **beschwören** is more solemn and urgent than everyday **bitten** or
**anflehen** — it's typically used when someone begs another with great intensity to reconsider a dangerous
or momentous decision. Don't confuse it with the compound **heraufbeschwören**, which specifically means to
summon something (often a bad outcome) into being through one's own words or actions, as in **eine Krise
heraufbeschwören** ("to bring about/precipitate a crisis").

## Bergungsoperation

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** A rescue, recovery, or salvage operation — an organized effort to retrieve people, vehicles,
wreckage, or valuable objects from a dangerous, inaccessible, or disaster-stricken location (e.g., after a
shipwreck, plane crash, avalanche, or mine collapse).

**Synonym:** **Rettungsaktion** (rescue operation/action, more general, focused on saving lives) ·
**Rettungseinsatz** (rescue mission/deployment, esp. by emergency services) · **Bergungsaktion** (near-synonym,
recovery action) · **Rettungsmission** (rescue mission)

**Grammar:** Feminine compound noun: **die Bergungsoperation**, plural **die Bergungsoperationen**. Formed
from **die Bergung** (rescue/recovery/salvage, from the verb **bergen** — to rescue/recover/salvage) + **die
Operation** (operation). Common collocations: **eine Bergungsoperation durchführen/starten/leiten** ("to carry
out/start/lead a recovery operation").

**Example:** *Die Bergungsoperation nach dem Schiffsunglück dauerte mehrere Tage.* — "The recovery operation
after the shipping accident lasted several days."

Another example: *Die Küstenwache leitete die Bergungsoperation für das gesunkene U-Boot.* — "The coast guard
led the recovery operation for the sunken submarine."

**English:** **rescue / recovery / salvage operation** · **French:** **opération de sauvetage / opération de
récupération**

Useful nuance: distinguish **Bergung** from **Rettung** — **Bergung** emphasizes retrieving or recovering
something or someone from a difficult physical situation, often after the fact, and can apply equally to
survivors or to bodies/wreckage; **Rettung** emphasizes saving a life and generally implies success. A
**Bergungsoperation** may be mounted to recover the deceased or debris, whereas a **Rettungsaktion** always
implies an attempt to save someone alive.

## Sünde

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Sin — a transgression against religious or moral law; a wrongful, immoral act, especially one
considered an offense against God or a moral/ethical code. Also used loosely and playfully for a minor
indulgence or guilty pleasure.

**Synonym:** **Vergehen** (offense/misdemeanor, more legal/neutral) · **Verfehlung** (a lapse/failing, softer)
· **Frevel** (sacrilege/outrage, more archaic/dramatic) · **Missetat** (misdeed, dated)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Sünde**, plural **die Sünden**. Related verb: **sündigen** (to sin).
Collocations: **eine Sünde begehen** ("to commit a sin"), **gegen etwas sündigen** ("to sin against
something"), **eine Todsünde** ("a mortal/deadly sin").

**Example:** *Er bereute seine Sünden und bat um Vergebung.* — "He repented his sins and asked for
forgiveness."

Another example: *Naschen zwischen den Mahlzeiten ist meine kleine Sünde.* — "Snacking between meals is my
little sin."

**English:** **sin** · **French:** **péché**

Useful nuance: **Sünde** spans a serious religious/moral register (**eine Todsünde**, "a mortal sin") down to
a light, joking use for an indulgence or vice (**eine kleine Sünde**, "a little sin," e.g., eating dessert).
Tone and context decide whether it's meant earnestly or tongue-in-cheek.

## aufbrechen

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** (intransitive) To set off, depart, get going on a journey; (intransitive) to burst open, crack
open, split open (of buds, wounds, ice); (transitive) to break open, force open, pry open (a door, lock, safe,
crate).

**Synonym:** for "depart": **losgehen, abreisen, sich auf den Weg machen** · for "force open": **aufsprengen,
knacken** (colloquial, esp. locks/safes), **gewaltsam öffnen**

**Grammar:** Separable, strong verb: **aufbrechen, brach auf, ist/hat aufgebrochen**. Auxiliary depends on
meaning: the intransitive senses (depart; burst open on its own) take **sein** — **ist aufgebrochen**; the
transitive sense (forcibly open something) takes **haben** — **hat aufgebrochen**, governing the accusative:
**eine Tür aufbrechen** ("to break open a door").

**Example:** *Wir sind früh am Morgen aufgebrochen, um den Stau zu vermeiden.* — "We set off early in the
morning to avoid the traffic jam."

Another example: *Die Einbrecher haben die Tür mit einem Brecheisen aufgebrochen.* — "The burglars broke open
the door with a crowbar."

**English:** **to set off/depart / to break open, force open / to burst open (buds, wounds)** · **French:**
**partir, se mettre en route / forcer, fracturer / éclore, s'ouvrir**

Useful nuance: the auxiliary tracks the meaning — **sein** for departure or something splitting open on its
own (**Die Knospen sind aufgebrochen**, "the buds have burst open"), **haben** when someone forcibly opens
something (**Er hat den Safe aufgebrochen**, "he broke open the safe"). Don't confuse with **ausbrechen** (to
break out/escape) or **abbrechen** (to break off, cancel).

## verlocken

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** To tempt, entice, allure, or tantalize — to make something seem attractive or desirable in a
way that draws someone toward an action, often one that's indulgent, risky, or otherwise not strictly
sensible.

**Synonym:** **verführen** (more seductive/morally loaded) · **reizen** (to appeal to/attract) · **ködern**
(colloquial, "to bait/lure") · **anlocken** (to lure/draw in, more neutral or physical) depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak, inseparable verb: **verlocken – verlockte – hat verlockt**. Construction: **jemanden zu
etwas (Dativ) verlocken** ("to tempt someone into something") or **etwas verlockt jemanden** ("something
tempts someone," with the temptation as grammatical subject). The present participle **verlockend** is
extremely common as a standalone adjective meaning "tempting/appealing" (**ein verlockendes Angebot**, "a
tempting offer"). Related noun: **die Verlockung, -en** ("temptation, allure, enticement").

**Example:** *Das gute Wetter verlockte uns zu einem Spaziergang.* — "The nice weather tempted us into a
walk."

Another example: *Es ist verlockend, jetzt einfach aufzugeben, aber wir sollten weitermachen.* — "It's
tempting to just give up now, but we should keep going."

**English:** **to tempt / entice / allure / tantalize** · **French:** **tenter / séduire / allécher**

Useful nuance: **verlocken** is more general than **verführen** — it describes something (an offer, the
weather, a price, an idea) making an option attractive, typically with an inanimate or abstract subject,
without the stronger moral or romantic/seductive overtone that **verführen** carries (which often implies
leading someone astray). The adjective **verlockend** shows up constantly in everyday German for anything
appealing — a deal, a smell, a plan — roughly parallel to English "tempting."

## aufzehren

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To use up completely, consume, deplete, or exhaust — typically said of a reserve such as
savings, supplies, strength, or patience that is gradually drawn down until nothing is left.

**Synonym:** **aufbrauchen** (to use up completely, close synonym, more neutral/administrative) ·
**verbrauchen** (to use up/consume, general) · **erschöpfen** (to exhaust) · **verzehren** (to consume,
especially food, same root) depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable weak verb: **aufzehren – zehrte auf – hat aufgezehrt**. Transitive: **etwas
(Akkusativ) aufzehren** (*die Vorräte aufzehren*, "to use up the supplies"). Built on the root verb **zehren**
("to live off/draw sustenance from," used with **von**: **von etwas zehren**, "to live off something," e.g.,
**von den Erinnerungen zehren**, "to live off one's memories").

**Example:** *Die Familie musste ihre Ersparnisse aufzehren, um die schwere Zeit zu überstehen.* — "The family
had to use up their savings to get through the hard time."

Another example: *Der lange Streit hat seine Geduld völlig aufgezehrt.* — "The long dispute completely
exhausted his patience."

**English:** **to use up / consume / deplete / exhaust** · **French:** **épuiser / consommer entièrement**

Useful nuance: **aufzehren** carries a sense of gradual, complete depletion of a finite reserve, and often
implies the process itself was draining or wearing — savings, strength, patience, supplies dwindling to
nothing. It's close to **aufbrauchen**, but **aufbrauchen** is more neutral/administrative (simply finishing
off a stock), while **aufzehren** more readily extends metaphorically to abstract things like patience,
energy, or morale being worn down.

## trügerisch

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Deceptive, deceiving, illusory, or treacherous — appearing safe, true, or favorable while
actually hiding something false or dangerous.

**Synonym:** **irreführend** (misleading) · **täuschend** (deceptive, close synonym) · **trügend** (rarer,
present-participle form of the same root verb) depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective, regularly declined (**trügerische Ruhe**, **ein trügerisches Gefühl**, **trügerisches
Eis**). Derived from the strong, mostly literary verb **trügen – trog – hat getrogen** ("to deceive," now
largely confined to set phrases like **wenn mich nicht alles trügt**, "if I'm not mistaken/if my instincts
don't deceive me"). Frequent fixed collocations: **trügerische Sicherheit** ("a false sense of security"),
**trügerische Ruhe/Stille** ("deceptive calm"), **trügerisches Eis** ("treacherous/unsafe-looking ice").

**Example:** *Die Ruhe vor dem Sturm ist oft trügerisch.* — "The calm before the storm is often deceptive."

Another example: *Man sollte sich nicht von trügerischer Sicherheit einlullen lassen.* — "One shouldn't be
lulled by a false sense of security."

**English:** **deceptive / misleading / illusory / treacherous** · **French:** **trompeur / illusoire**

Useful nuance: **trügerisch** describes something whose appearance misleads about its true, often dangerous,
nature — thin ice that looks solid, a calm that precedes danger, a sense of security with no real basis. Don't
confuse it with **betrügerisch** ("fraudulent," from **betrügen**, "to cheat/defraud someone"), which carries
a moral or criminal charge about a person's intent, whereas **trügerisch** is about a thing or situation being
inherently misleading rather than about someone actively deceiving.

## überstürzt

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Hasty, rash, precipitate, or rushed — done too quickly and without sufficient care, thought,
or preparation.

**Synonym:** **hastig** (hasty, hurried) · **vorschnell** (premature/rash) · **unüberlegt** (ill-considered) ·
**Hals über Kopf** (colloquial idiom, "headlong/in a mad rush") depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective/past participle of the weak, inseparable verb **überstürzen – überstürzte – hat
überstürzt**. Transitive: **etwas überstürzen** ("to rush something"), very often in the negative imperative
**nichts überstürzen** ("not to rush anything," "let's not be hasty"). Reflexive: **sich überstürzen**, said of
events, to tumble over one another in rapid succession (**die Ereignisse überstürzten sich**, "events
unfolded in rapid succession"). As an attributive adjective: **eine überstürzte Entscheidung** ("a rash/hasty
decision").

**Example:** *Er bereute seine überstürzte Entscheidung, das Angebot abzulehnen.* — "He regretted his hasty
decision to turn down the offer."

Another example: *Die Ereignisse überstürzten sich nach der Ankündigung.* — "Events tumbled over one another
after the announcement."

**English:** **hasty / rash / precipitate / rushed** · **French:** **précipité / hâtif**

Useful nuance: keep the three uses distinct — the adjective **überstürzt** describes a hasty decision or
action taken without due care; the reflexive **sich überstürzen** describes events or developments piling up
quickly one after another (not a person rushing); and the transitive **etwas überstürzen** describes a person
rushing a process, most commonly heard as the warning **nichts überstürzen**, "don't rush it."

## wittern

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To scent, sniff out, or sense — literally, of an animal detecting prey or danger by smell;
figuratively, to intuitively sense or suspect something (an opportunity, danger, betrayal) often before it is
confirmed or obvious.

**Synonym:** **riechen** (to smell, more literal) · **spüren** (to sense/feel) · **ahnen** (to have an
inkling, to suspect) · **vermuten** (to presume/suspect) depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak verb: **wittern – witterte – hat gewittert**. Transitive: **etwas wittern**. Very common
collocations: **Gefahr wittern** ("to sense danger"), **eine Chance/Gelegenheit wittern** ("to sense/sniff out
an opportunity"), **Verrat wittern** ("to suspect betrayal"), **Morgenluft wittern** (idiom, literally "to
scent morning air," meaning to sense that circumstances are turning in one's favor and to seize the moment).
Related noun: **die Witterung** ("weather" in general use, but also a hunting term for a scent trail — **die
Witterung aufnehmen**, "to pick up the scent").

**Example:** *Der Wolf witterte die Beute schon aus großer Entfernung.* — "The wolf scented the prey from a
great distance already."

Another example: *Die Konkurrenz witterte sofort eine Chance, als das Unternehmen ins Wanken geriet.* — "The
competition immediately sensed an opportunity when the company began to falter."

**English:** **to scent / sniff out / sense / smell (an opportunity, danger)** · **French:** **flairer /
sentir**

Useful nuance: **wittern** almost always implies an intuitive, pre-emptive detection — picking up on subtle
cues before something becomes overt — much like the English idioms "to smell blood" or "to sniff out an
opportunity." It's most at home with abstract objects like **Gefahr**, **Chance**, or **Verrat**, where it
signals sharp, almost animal instinct rather than calm deduction.

## seinerseits

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** For his/its part, on his/its side, in turn — used to point back to a masculine or neuter
subject's own contribution, reaction, or perspective, typically when setting it alongside or against another
party's action.

**Synonym:** **von sich aus** (of his own accord, slightly different nuance) · **im Gegenzug** (in return/in
turn) · **auf seiner Seite** (more literal paraphrase) depending on context

**Grammar:** Adverb, part of a family of possessive-based forms built with **-seits** (from **die Seite**,
"side"): **meinerseits** (for my part), **deinerseits** (informal "for your part"), **seinerseits** (for
his/its part), **ihrerseits** (for her/their part), **unsererseits** (for our part), **eurerseits** (informal
plural "for your part"), **Ihrerseits** (formal "for your part"). Each form agrees with the grammatical gender
and number of the noun or pronoun it refers back to, not necessarily natural gender — e.g., **das
Unternehmen ... seinerseits** ("the company ... for its part"), since **Unternehmen** is neuter. Mainly used
in formal or written register, often in two-clause contrastive constructions.

**Example:** *Er hatte hart gearbeitet; sein Chef erkannte das seinerseits an.* — "He had worked hard; his
boss, for his part, acknowledged it."

Another example: *Der Konzern kündigte Kürzungen an; der Betriebsrat reagierte seinerseits mit Protest.* —
"The corporation announced cuts; the works council, for its part, responded with protest."

**English:** **for his/its part / on his/its side / in turn** · **French:** **de son côté / pour sa part**

Useful nuance: choosing the right member of the **-seits** family means matching the grammatical gender/number
of whoever's "side" is being highlighted, not the natural gender of a person necessarily — a neuter noun like
**das Ministerium** takes **seinerseits**, a feminine or plural one takes **ihrerseits**. The whole family
signals a kind of reciprocal or parallel structure: one party did X, and this party, in turn/for its part, did
Y.

## nachgeben

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** To give in, yield, relent, or give way — said of a person ceding to pressure, persuasion, or
demands after resistance; or of a material or structure physically giving way, sagging, or buckling under
weight or force.

**Synonym:** for a person: **einlenken** (to come around/relent) · **sich beugen** (to submit/bow to) ·
**kapitulieren** (to capitulate); for a material: **einbrechen** (to cave in, more sudden) · **sich
verformen** (to deform) depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable strong verb: **nachgeben – gab nach – hat nachgegeben**. Intransitive, usually with a
dative object for the person/thing pressuring: **jemandem/etwas (Dativ) nachgeben** (*dem Druck nachgeben*,
"to give in to the pressure"; *einer Bitte nachgeben*, "to give in to a request"). Also used absolutely,
without a dative, for materials or structures: **das Eis/der Boden gibt nach** ("the ice/ground gives way").

**Example:** *Nach langem Streiten gab die Mutter schließlich nach und erlaubte den Ausflug.* — "After a long
argument, the mother finally gave in and allowed the trip."

Another example: *Das morsche Holz gab unter seinem Gewicht nach.* — "The rotten wood gave way under his
weight."

**English:** **to give in / yield / relent / give way** · **French:** **céder / plier**

Useful nuance: **nachgeben** implies there was some resistance or a conflict of wills first, and one side
relents under continued pressure or persuasion — distinct from **aufgeben** ("to give up/quit"), which means
abandoning an effort or goal altogether, with no requirement that anyone was pushing back. You can **einer
Bitte nachgeben** without ever having wanted to **aufgeben** anything.

Useful nuance: because it's built on **weit** ("wide"), **ausweiten** evokes something being stretched wider

tard**

Useful nuance: **aufsparen** implies a deliberate, often strategic reserving of something — money, energy, a

## gereizt

**CEFR:** roughly **B1/B2**.

**Definition:** Irritated, irritable, on edge, or tetchy — describing a person's mood as easily annoyed or

a temporary state) · **angespannt** (tense) · **nervös** (nervous, edgy) depending on context

provoke, stimulate; also to tempt/entice"). As a predicative or attributive adjective, regularly declined and
compared: **gereizt – gereizter – am gereiztesten**. Common uses: **gereizt sein** ("to be irritated/on

Useful nuance: **gereizt** describes a temporary state — irritated right now, often from stress, fatigue, or a
specific trigger — as opposed to **reizbar**, which names a lasting character trait of being easily provoked.

## umgehen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A minimal pair of two verbs distinguished only by stress and separability: **(1) separable
"úmgehen"** — to handle, deal with, treat, or manage a person, situation, or tool (**mit etwas/jemandem
umgehen**), and, in older/idiomatic use, "to go around/circulate" (of rumors) or "to haunt" (of a ghost).
**(2) inseparable "umgéhen"** — to circumvent, bypass, evade, or get around a law, rule, obstacle, or
question (**etwas umgehen**).

**Synonym:** separable sense: **behandeln, sich verhalten zu** (to treat/handle) · inseparable sense:
**umschiffen, sich entziehen, ausweichen** (to sidestep/dodge) depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable, strong verb: **umgehen – ging um – ist umgegangen**; construction **mit jemandem/etwas
(Dativ) umgehen**; infinitive with **zu** splits the prefix: **umzugehen**. Inseparable, strong verb: **umgehen
– umging – hat umgangen**; construction **etwas (Akkusativ) umgehen**; infinitive with **zu** stays whole,
since the prefix never separates: **zu umgehen**. The two are told apart in speech by stress (**Úmgehen** vs.
**umgéhen**) and in writing by their different past participles and auxiliaries.

**Example:** *Sie weiß, wie man mit schwierigen Kunden umgeht.* — "She knows how to deal with difficult
customers." (separable)

Another example: *Die Firma versuchte, die neuen Vorschriften zu umgehen.* — "The company tried to circumvent
the new regulations." (inseparable)

**English:** separable: **to handle / deal with / treat**; inseparable: **to circumvent / bypass / evade /
get around** · **French:** separable: **traiter / gérer**; inseparable: **contourner / éluder**

Useful nuance: this is one of the classic German minimal pairs where the same spelling hides two unrelated
meanings depending on whether the prefix is separable or not. A quick test: if it can take **mit + Dativ**
and forms **ist umgegangen**, it's the "handle/deal with" verb; if it takes a direct accusative object and
forms **hat umgangen**, it's the "circumvent/evade" verb.

**CEFR:** roughly **B2/C1**.

**Definition:** To kindle or ignite — literally, to start a fire or flame (**ein Feuer entfachen**); much more
commonly used figuratively, to spark, stir up, or trigger something intense — enthusiasm, passion, hope, a
conflict, a debate, or a crisis — bringing it suddenly and forcefully into being.

**Synonym:** **literal sense: anzünden / entzünden**; **figurative sense: auslösen / hervorrufen / schüren

**English:** **to kindle / ignite / spark / stir up** · **French:** **enflammer / attiser / susciter**

Useful nuance: **Entfachen** almost always implies something sudden and forceful bursting into being — a fire
catching, a debate erupting, enthusiasm surging — closer to "ignite" than to the more gradual **wecken**
("to awaken," see [[erwecken]]), which suggests calling forth something latent or dormant. It pairs naturally
with dramatic, often journalistic subjects: a single remark, image, or event that **entfacht** a controversy,

## enttäuschen

**CEFR:** roughly **A2/B1**.

**Definition:** To disappoint — to fail to meet someone's hopes, expectations, or trust, leaving them let down;
can describe people, events, results, or products falling short of what was expected or hoped for.

**Synonym:** **frustrieren** (to frustrate, more about thwarted effort than dashed hope) · **ernüchtern**
(to sober/disillusion, softer, more about a reality check) · **im Stich lassen** (to let someone down/abandon,
stronger, relational) depending on context

**Grammar:** Weak, inseparable verb: **enttäuschen, enttäuschte, hat enttäuscht**. Governs the accusative:
**jemanden (Akkusativ) enttäuschen**. Past participle **enttäuscht** is also a common adjective ("disappointed"),
used with **sein** (*ich bin enttäuscht*) or **von** (*enttäuscht von etwas/jemandem*, "disappointed
by/in something/someone"). Related noun: **die Enttäuschung** ("disappointment"). Common collocations:
**jemandes Erwartungen enttäuschen** ("to disappoint someone's expectations"), **bitter enttäuscht sein**
("to be bitterly disappointed").

**Example:** *Der neue Film hat mich leider enttäuscht.* — "Unfortunately, the new film disappointed me."

Another example: *Sie war enttäuscht von seiner Reaktion auf die gute Nachricht.* — "She was disappointed by
his reaction to the good news."

**English:** **to disappoint** · **French:** **décevoir**

Useful nuance: **Enttäuschen** centers on the gap between expectation and outcome — someone hoped for X and
got less. The adjectival participle **enttäuscht** is extremely common in everyday speech (*ich bin so
enttäuscht*), often paired with **von** to name the source. Compare **frustrieren**, which centers more on
blocked effort or ongoing annoyance than on a single dashed hope.

## Stimme

**CEFR:** roughly **A2**.

**Definition:** Voice — the sound a person makes when speaking or singing; also, by extension, a vote (as in
an election or ballot) and, more figuratively, an opinion or viewpoint that is "heard" (e.g., "eine innere
Stimme," an inner voice).

**Synonym:** none direct for the vocal sense (**Stimme** is the standard term); for the "vote" sense: **die
Wahlstimme** (more specific, "electoral vote"); for the "voice/opinion" sense: **die Meinung** (opinion, less
literal) depending on context

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Stimme**, plural **die Stimmen**. Related verb: **stimmen** ("to be
correct/right," also "to vote," also "to tune" an instrument); related adjective: **stimmhaft** ("voiced,"
phonetics). Common collocations: **die Stimme erheben** ("to raise one's voice"), **seine Stimme abgeben**
("to cast one's vote"), **mit lauter/leiser Stimme** ("in a loud/quiet voice"), **eine innere Stimme**
("an inner voice/gut feeling"), **stimmberechtigt** ("entitled to vote").

**Example:** *Ihre Stimme zitterte vor Aufregung, als sie die Ergebnisse verkündete.* — "Her voice trembled
with excitement as she announced the results."

Another example: *Bei der Abstimmung gab jeder Bürger seine Stimme ab.* — "In the vote, every citizen cast
their vote."

**English:** **voice / vote** · **French:** **voix**

Useful nuance: **Stimme** neatly bridges the literal sense (the physical voice) and the political/procedural
sense (a vote), much like French **voix** and English "voice" in phrases like "make your voice heard" —
German extends this into the compound **Abstimmung** ("vote/ballot," literally "a tuning/aligning of voices")
and the verb **stimmen**, which on its own means "to be correct," reflecting the idea of voices being "in
tune" or agreement.
