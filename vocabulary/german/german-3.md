## die Ausgewogenheit

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** Balance or equilibrium — the quality of being well-proportioned, fair, or evenly weighted, in
the sense of a diet, a report, an argument, a portfolio, or a person's temperament containing no excessive or
one-sided element.

**Synonym:** **das Gleichgewicht / die Balance / die Objektivität (for reporting/argument)** depending on
context

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Ausgewogenheit**; usually uncountable, no plural. Derived from the adjective
**ausgewogen** ("balanced"), itself the past participle of the separable verb **etwas (Akkusativ) auswiegen**
("to weigh out/counterbalance"). Common constructions: **auf Ausgewogenheit achten** ("to pay attention to
balance"), **die Ausgewogenheit der Berichterstattung** ("the balance of the reporting"), **die Ausgewogenheit
einer Ernährung** ("the balance of a diet").

**Example:** *Die Redaktion legt großen Wert auf die Ausgewogenheit ihrer Berichterstattung.* — "The editorial
team places great importance on the balance of its reporting."

Another example: *Eine gute Ausgewogenheit zwischen Arbeit und Freizeit ist wichtig für die Gesundheit.* — "A
good balance between work and leisure is important for health."

**English:** **balance / equilibrium / even-handedness** · **French:** **équilibre / impartialité**

Useful nuance: **Ausgewogenheit** is often used for something that should fairly represent multiple sides or
elements — a balanced diet, balanced media coverage, a balanced argument — with a connotation of deliberate
fairness or proportion, closer to "even-handedness" than the more general **Gleichgewicht**, which is the
standard word for "equilibrium" in physical, ecological, or political contexts (*das ökologische
Gleichgewicht*, "the ecological balance").

## durchsetzen / sich (Akkusativ) durchsetzen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To enforce, push through, or successfully implement something, such as a demand, plan, law, or
one's will (**etwas durchsetzen**); reflexively, to prevail, assert oneself, or gain acceptance — said of a
person, idea, technology, or trend that wins out against resistance or competition (**sich durchsetzen**).

**Synonym:** **etwas durchsetzen: erzwingen / durchdrücken / durchbringen**; **sich durchsetzen: sich behaupten
/ Erfolg haben / sich etablieren** depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable weak verb: **durchsetzen – setzte durch – hat durchgesetzt**. Transitive: **etwas
(Akkusativ) [gegen jemanden/etwas (Akkusativ)] durchsetzen** (*seine Forderungen gegen den Widerstand
durchsetzen*, "to push one's demands through against resistance"). Reflexive: **sich (Akkusativ) [gegen
jemanden/etwas (Akkusativ)] durchsetzen** (*sich gegen die Konkurrenz durchsetzen*, "to prevail over the
competition"). The prefix separates in a main clause: *Er setzt sich durch*; infinitive with **zu** is
**durchzusetzen**. Note: an inseparable, differently stressed verb **durchsétzen** (past participle
**durchsetzt**, not **durchgesetzt**) exists with the unrelated meaning "to permeate/intersperse something
with something" (*ein Gestein, das mit Quarz durchsetzt ist*, "rock interspersed with quartz") — a different
word from the same root, distinguished by stress and by its participle.

**Example:** *Die Gewerkschaft konnte ihre Forderungen nach höheren Löhnen durchsetzen.* — "The union was able
to push through its demands for higher wages."

Another example: *Das neue Modell hat sich schnell auf dem Markt durchgesetzt.* — "The new model quickly
prevailed/caught on in the market."

**English:** **to enforce / to push through / to implement**; **sich durchsetzen: to prevail / to assert
oneself / to win out** · **French:** **imposer / faire valoir**; **sich durchsetzen: s'imposer / l'emporter**

Useful nuance: **Durchsetzen** (transitive) emphasizes successfully making something happen despite obstacles
or opposition — a policy, a demand, one's will. **Sich durchsetzen** (reflexive) shifts the focus to a person,
idea, or product itself winning out or gaining acceptance, often in competition with alternatives. Don't
confuse either with **sich einsetzen [für etwas]** ("to advocate/campaign for something"), which describes
effort toward a goal without necessarily implying success.

## jemanden von etwas (Dativ) abbringen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To dissuade or talk someone out of doing something, or to divert someone from a plan, opinion,
or course of action, usually through persuasion or argument.

**Synonym:** **jemanden von etwas überzeugen, es nicht zu tun / jemandem etwas ausreden / jemanden umstimmen**
depending on context

**Grammar:** Separable strong verb: **abbringen – brachte ab – hat abgebracht**. Construction: **jemanden
(Akkusativ) von etwas (Dativ) abbringen** (*ihn von seinem Plan abbringen*, "to talk him out of his plan").
Often used in the negative to express that someone cannot be dissuaded: **sich von etwas nicht abbringen
lassen** ("to not let oneself be talked out of something"). The prefix separates in a main clause: *Sie bringt
ihn davon ab*; infinitive with **zu** is **abzubringen**.

**Example:** *Seine Freunde konnten ihn nicht von seinem Vorhaben abbringen.* — "His friends could not talk him
out of his plan."

Another example: *Sie ließ sich von niemandem von ihrer Meinung abbringen.* — "She would not let anyone talk
her out of her opinion."

**English:** **to dissuade / to talk out of / to divert from** · **French:** **dissuader / détourner de**

Useful nuance: **Abbringen** always specifies what someone is being diverted *from* with **von + Dativ** — a
plan, a decision, an opinion, a path. The common construction **sich nicht abbringen lassen** ("to not be
dissuaded/deterred") emphasizes stubborn resolve. It contrasts with **überreden** ("to persuade someone *to*
do something"), which points the other direction.

## bedächtig

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Deliberate, careful, and unhurried in manner, thought, speech, or movement — thinking things
through before acting or speaking, often at a measured, slow pace. Depending on context it can sound either
positive (thoughtful, prudent) or mildly critical (overly slow, ponderous).

**Synonym:** **besonnen / überlegt / bedachtsam / langsam und überlegt** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective, also used adverbially: *eine bedächtige Antwort*, *Er sprach bedächtig*. Regular
inflection before a noun according to case, gender, number, and determiner: **bedächtig, bedächtiger, am
bedächtigsten**. Related to the verb **bedenken** ("to consider, think over") and the adjective **bedacht**
("mindful, careful," often in **auf etwas (Akkusativ) bedacht sein**, "to be intent on/mindful of something").

**Example:** *Der alte Mann ging langsam und bedächtig über den Hof.* — "The old man walked slowly and
deliberately across the yard."

Another example: *Sie antwortete bedächtig, nachdem sie kurz nachgedacht hatte.* — "She answered thoughtfully,
after considering briefly."

**English:** **deliberate / thoughtful / measured / unhurried** · **French:** **posé / réfléchi / mesuré**

Useful nuance: **Bedächtig** emphasizes a slow, considered manner — someone who thinks before acting or
speaking, whether admiringly ("prudent, careful") or with a hint of impatience ("ponderous, slow"). **Besonnen**
leans more purely positive, stressing level-headedness and composure, especially in a crisis, while
**bedächtig** more strongly evokes physical or verbal slowness alongside the thoughtfulness.
