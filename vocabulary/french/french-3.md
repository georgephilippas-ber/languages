## la faillite / faillites en chaîne

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Faillites en chaîne** means a series of business failures or bankruptcies occurring one after another,
often because the failure of one company causes others to fail. **Faillite** on its own means bankruptcy or,
figuratively, the complete failure of a policy, system, or undertaking.

**Synonym:** **faillites en cascade** (cascading bankruptcies); **une vague de faillites** (a wave of bankruptcies,
without necessarily implying that one causes another); **des défaillances d’entreprises** (business failures, a broader
economic term)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la faillite**, plural **les faillites**. The expression normally occurs in the plural:
**des faillites en chaîne**. **En chaîne** is invariable and means “in succession” or “in a chain reaction.” Common
combinations include **provoquer / entraîner des faillites en chaîne** (“to cause / lead to cascading bankruptcies”),
**une série de faillites en chaîne** (“a series of cascading bankruptcies”), and **faire faillite** (“to go bankrupt”).
In **faire faillite**, there is no article before **faillite**: **l’entreprise fait faillite**, **elle a fait
faillite**.

**Example:** *La fermeture de cette grande entreprise a provoqué des faillites en chaîne chez ses fournisseurs.* — “The
closure of this large company caused a chain of bankruptcies among its suppliers.”

Another example: *Les banques craignent des faillites en chaîne si la crise s’aggrave.* — “Banks fear cascading
bankruptcies if the crisis worsens.”

**English:** **cascading bankruptcies / a chain of business failures** · **German:** **eine Pleitewelle / eine Kette von
Unternehmensinsolvenzen**

Useful nuance: **En chaîne** often suggests a domino effect: one company fails, cannot pay its suppliers, and puts those
suppliers at risk of failing too. It can also describe failures occurring in rapid succession without establishing a
direct causal link. **Faillite** is common in everyday language and journalism; precise legal terms depend on the
jurisdiction and may include **cessation des paiements**, **redressement judiciaire**, or **liquidation judiciaire**,
which are not interchangeable. Figuratively, **la faillite d’un système** means “the failure of a system,” not financial
bankruptcy.

## le licenciement

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Licenciement** means the termination of an employee’s employment contract by the employer: a dismissal
or, especially for economic reasons, a layoff. **Licenciements** is the plural form.

**Synonym:** **renvoi** (dismissal, less formal and less legally precise); **congédiement** (dismissal, especially
common in Canadian French)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le licenciement**, plural **les licenciements**. Common combinations include **un
licenciement économique** (“a redundancy / layoff for economic reasons”), **un licenciement pour faute grave**
(“dismissal for serious misconduct”), **des licenciements collectifs** (“collective dismissals / mass layoffs”), **une
vague de licenciements** (“a wave of layoffs”), and **procéder à des licenciements** (“to carry out layoffs /
dismissals”). The related verb is **licencier quelqu’un** (“to dismiss / lay someone off”), a regular **-er** verb: **il
licencie**, **il a licencié**. The passive construction **être licencié(e)** means “to be dismissed / laid off.”

**Example:** *L’entreprise a annoncé une vague de licenciements.* — “The company announced a wave of layoffs.”

Another example: *Elle a contesté son licenciement devant le conseil de prud’hommes.* — “She challenged her dismissal
before the employment tribunal.”

**English:** **dismissal / layoff / redundancy** · **German:** **Entlassung / Kündigung durch den Arbeitgeber**

Useful nuance: **Licenciement** is initiated by the employer, unlike **démission** (“resignation”), which is initiated
by the employee. It does not necessarily imply misconduct: **un licenciement économique** results from economic
circumstances, whereas **un licenciement pour motif personnel** concerns reasons relating to the employee. English
“layoff” usually suggests an economic reason; “dismissal” is broader. **Suppression de postes** means “elimination of
positions” and does not always involve dismissing employees. Despite the shared word family, **licenciement** does not
mean obtaining a **licence** (“degree / licence”).

## printanier

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Printanier** means relating to spring or characteristic of spring, especially its mild weather,
flowers, and fresh colours. Figuratively, it can suggest freshness or youthful charm. **Printanières** is the feminine
plural form.

**Synonym:** **du printemps** (of spring; the usual equivalent); **vernal** (relating to spring, literary or technical)

**Grammar:** Adjective: masculine singular **printanier**, feminine singular **printanière**, masculine plural
**printaniers**, feminine plural **printanières**. It agrees in gender and number with the noun it describes and usually
follows it. Common combinations include **des fleurs printanières** (“spring flowers”), **des températures
printanières** (“springlike temperatures”), **une douceur printanière** (“springlike mildness”), and **une ambiance
printanière** (“a springlike atmosphere”).

**Example:** *Les températures printanières nous donnent envie de sortir.* — “The springlike temperatures make us want
to go outside.”

Another example: *Elle a décoré la table avec des fleurs printanières.* — “She decorated the table with spring flowers.”

**English:** **spring / springlike** · **German:** **Frühlings- / frühlingshaft**

Useful nuance: **Printanier** can describe something that occurs in spring or something that merely resembles spring.
**Des températures printanières** can therefore occur in winter if the weather is unusually mild. **Du printemps** is
often interchangeable with it, but **printanier** more readily evokes the season’s characteristic freshness and
atmosphere.
