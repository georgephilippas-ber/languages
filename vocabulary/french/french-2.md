## le chef-d’œuvre

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A work regarded as the finest, most accomplished, or most representative achievement of an artist,
writer, craftsperson, or creator. More generally, it can mean an exceptionally excellent or skillful achievement.

**Synonym:** **l’œuvre maîtresse / la pièce maîtresse / la réalisation exceptionnelle**

**Grammar:** Masculine compound noun: **le chef-d’œuvre**; plural: **les chefs-d’œuvre**. Only **chef**
takes the plural marker; **œuvre** stays singular. Common constructions include **un chef-d’œuvre de + creator or field** (*un chef-d’œuvre de la
peinture*), **considérer quelque chose comme un chef-d’œuvre** (“to consider something a masterpiece”), and **être un
chef-d’œuvre** (“to be a masterpiece”).

**Example:** *Ce tableau est considéré comme un chef-d’œuvre de la peinture
française.* — “This painting is considered a masterpiece of French painting.”

Another example: *Le film est un véritable chef-d’œuvre.* — “The film is a true masterpiece.”

**English:** **masterpiece / magnum opus** · **German:** **das Meisterwerk / das Hauptwerk**

Useful nuance: The literal components mean “master” and “work,” but the established meaning is **masterpiece**. The
plural is **chefs-d’œuvre**, not *chefs-d’œuvres*.

## de fait

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** An invariable
adverbial expression meaning **in fact**, **as a matter of fact**, or **in reality**. In formal, legal, or political
contexts, it can mean **de facto**, describing a situation that exists in practice even if it is not officially
recognized or stated.

**Synonym:** **en fait / effectivement / dans les faits / de facto** depending on context

**Grammar:** Fixed adverbial phrase: **de fait**. It can appear at the beginning of a sentence,
often followed by a comma: *De fait, la décision a changé la situation*. It can also follow the verb or clause: *Il est
de fait responsable de l’équipe* (“He is de facto responsible for the team”). Do not confuse it with **un fait** (“a
fact”), which is a masculine noun.

**Example:** *De fait, personne n’a contesté cette décision.* — “In fact, nobody
challenged that decision.”

Another example: *Elle est devenue, de fait, la dirigeante du groupe.* — “She became, in practice, the leader of the
group.”

**English:** **in fact / actually / in practice / de facto** · **German:** **tatsächlich / faktisch / de facto**

Useful nuance: **En fait** is common in everyday speech and can introduce a correction or clarification. **De fait**
sounds more formal and emphasizes what is established by reality or practice, especially in legal, administrative, and
analytical writing.

## confier quelqu’un/quelque chose à quelqu’un / se confier à quelqu’un

**CEFR:** roughly **B1–B2**.

**Definition:** To entrust a person or thing to
someone’s care, responsibility, or protection; to assign a task or role; or to tell someone a confidence or private
matter. Reflexively, **se confier à quelqu’un** means to confide in or open up to someone.

**Synonym:** **remettre / charger / révéler** depending on context

**Grammar:** Regular **-er**
verb: **confier – confia – a confié**. **Confié** is the masculine singular past participle; the feminine and plural
forms are **confiée, confiés, confiées**. The standard construction is **confier quelque chose/quelqu’un (direct object) à
quelqu’un (indirect object introduced by à)**: *confier un dossier à un collègue*, *confier son enfant à sa sœur*. For
private information, use **confier quelque chose à quelqu’un** or **confier à quelqu’un que + clause**. The pronominal
form is **se confier à quelqu’un**.

**Example:** *La directrice lui a confié la gestion du projet.* — “The director
entrusted him with managing the project.”

Another example: *Elle s’est confiée à sa meilleure amie.* — “She confided in her best friend.”

**English:** **entrust / assign / confide / disclose**; participle: **entrusted / confided** · **German:** **anvertrauen
/ übertragen / sich jemandem anvertrauen**

Useful nuance: **Confier** always involves giving someone responsibility, care, or private information. **Donner**
simply means “give,” while **révéler** focuses on making information known and does not necessarily imply trust or a
recipient’s responsibility.

## aléatoire

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Determined by chance rather
than by a predictable plan, pattern, or intention; random or probabilistic. It can also describe something whose outcome
is uncertain or difficult to predict.

**Synonym:** **hasardeux / fortuit / imprévisible** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective with the same spelling in the masculine and feminine
singular: **aléatoire**; plural: **aléatoires**. Common constructions include **un choix aléatoire**, **une sélection
aléatoire**, **de manière aléatoire**, and **dépendre du hasard**. The related adverb is **aléatoirement** (“randomly”).

**Example:** *Les participants ont été répartis de manière aléatoire dans les deux groupes.* — “The participants were
assigned randomly to the two groups.”

Another example: *Le résultat de l’expérience reste aléatoire.* — “The result of the experiment remains unpredictable.”

**English:** **random / randomised / chance / unpredictable** · **German:** **zufällig / willkürlich / unvorhersehbar**

Useful nuance: In scientific or technical contexts, **aléatoire** usually means “random” or “probabilistic.” In everyday
contexts, it can suggest that something is unpredictable or haphazard. **Hasardeux** emphasizes risk, while
**arbitraire** means based on personal choice or without a justified rule, not simply random.

## révélateur / révélatrice

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** As an adjective, telling,
revealing, or indicative of something otherwise hidden — a sign that exposes an underlying truth, trait, or situation.
As a masculine noun, **le révélateur** refers to a developing agent or developer solution used in photography, or more
generally something that brings a hidden reality to light.

**Synonym:** **significatif / éloquent / indicateur** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective: **révélateur** (masculine), **révélatrice** (feminine); plural: **révélateurs / révélatrices**.
Common construction: **être révélateur de quelque
chose** (“to be revealing of something”). As a noun, masculine: **le révélateur**; plural: **les révélateurs**.

**Example:** *Son silence fut révélateur de son malaise.* — “Her silence was revealing of her unease.”

Another example: *Cette erreur est révélatrice d’un problème plus profond.* — “This mistake is indicative of a deeper
problem.”

**English:** **revealing / telling / indicative**; noun: **developer (photography) / revealer** · **German:** **aufschlussreich / bezeichnend / verräterisch**; noun: **der Entwickler**

Useful nuance: **Révélateur** as an adjective is often followed by **de + noun** to specify what is being revealed. It
is stronger and more literary than **significatif**, and unlike **éloquent**, it does not imply that something was said
— only that it exposes an underlying truth through its mere occurrence.

## enlisé / être enlisé dans quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Enlisé** means stuck in mud, sand, or other soft ground. Figuratively, it means bogged down in a
difficult situation and unable to make progress. **Enlisés** is the masculine plural form.

**Synonym:** **embourbé** (stuck in mud; also figuratively bogged down); **bloqué** (stuck or blocked, more general);
**empêtré** (entangled or caught up in difficulties)

**Grammar:** Past participle of **enliser**, also used as an adjective: **enlisé** (masculine singular), **enlisée**
(feminine singular), **enlisés** (masculine plural), **enlisées** (feminine plural). Common constructions are **être
enlisé dans quelque chose** (“to be bogged down in something”) and **s’enliser dans quelque chose** (“to get bogged down
in something”). The reflexive verb is regular: **s’enliser – il s’enlise – il s’enlisait – il s’est enlisé**. Its
compound tenses use **être**, with agreement with the subject: **elles se sont enlisées**. Common combinations include
**s’enliser dans la boue / le sable / un conflit / des négociations**. Non-reflexive **enliser quelque chose** means to
cause something to become stuck or bogged down.

**Example:** *Les véhicules sont enlisés dans la boue.* — “The vehicles are stuck in the mud.”

Another example: *Nous sommes enlisés dans des discussions sans fin.* — “We are bogged down in endless discussions.”

**English:** **stuck in mud or sand / bogged down** · **German:** **im Schlamm oder Sand festgefahren / festgefahren**

Useful nuance: **Être enlisé** describes a state, whereas **s’enliser** describes getting stuck or gradually losing
momentum. Figuratively, it often suggests prolonged difficulties rather than a brief obstacle: **les négociations
s’enlisent** means “the negotiations are getting bogged down.” Unlike the more general **bloqué**, **enlisé** evokes
being trapped in something that makes movement or progress increasingly difficult.

## la débâcle

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** **Débâcle** means a crushing defeat, a disorderly retreat, or a disastrous failure involving collapse or
disorganization. In its literal sense, it refers to the breaking up and movement of ice on a river during a thaw.

**Synonym:** **déroute** (rout, especially a military defeat or a crushing loss); **fiasco** (disastrous failure);
**effondrement** (collapse)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la débâcle**, plural **les débâcles**. Common combinations include **une débâcle militaire
/ électorale / financière** (“a military rout / electoral disaster / financial collapse”), **subir une débâcle** (“to
suffer a crushing defeat”), and **tourner à la débâcle** (“to turn into a disaster”). The literal sense appears in **la
débâcle des glaces** (“the breakup of the ice”).

**Example:** *Le parti a subi une véritable débâcle aux élections.* — “The party suffered a crushing defeat in the
elections.”

Another example: *Au printemps, la débâcle des glaces peut provoquer des inondations.* — “In spring, the breakup of the
ice can cause flooding.”

**English:** **rout / disastrous failure / ice breakup** · **German:** **vernichtende Niederlage / Fiasko / Eisgang**

Useful nuance: **Débâcle** is stronger than **défaite** (“defeat”) or **échec** (“failure”): it suggests a major
collapse, often accompanied by confusion or loss of control. It is common in journalism when describing military,
political, sporting, or financial disasters. In the literal sense, **débâcle** contrasts with **embâcle**, which refers
to an accumulation of ice or other material obstructing a watercourse.

## la requérante

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** A **requérante** is a woman who submits a formal request or application to an authority, or who brings a
case or appeal before a court. The word is mainly used in legal and administrative contexts. As an adjective,
**requérante** means “applying” or “petitioning,” as in **la partie requérante** (“the applicant party”).

**Synonym:** **demandeuse** (female applicant, more general); **demanderesse** (female claimant or plaintiff in legal
proceedings, depending on the procedure)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la requérante**, plural **les requérantes**. Masculine: **le requérant**, plural **les
requérants**. It is also an adjective: **requérant / requérante / requérants / requérantes**. Common combinations
include **la requérante demande que…** (“the applicant requests that…”), followed by the subjunctive, **la requérante a
déposé une requête** (“the applicant has filed an application”), and **la partie requérante** (“the applicant party”).
The related verb is **requérir** (“to request formally / to require”): **requérir – requiert – a requis**.

**Example:** *La requérante a déposé une demande d’asile.* — “The applicant submitted an asylum application.”

Another example: *La Cour a donné raison à la requérante.* — “The Court ruled in favour of the applicant.”

**English:** **female applicant / claimant / petitioner** · **German:** **Antragstellerin / Beschwerdeführerin**

Useful nuance: **Requérante** identifies a woman's procedural role, not necessarily someone making a complaint or
accusing another person. The best English translation depends on the institution and procedure: often “applicant,” but
sometimes “claimant” or “petitioner.” In everyday contexts, **demandeuse** or a more specific term such as **candidate**
is usually more natural. **Requérante** is not the ordinary French equivalent of “requiring” or “demanding” when
describing a task or a person's character.

## la comparution

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** **Comparution** means a person's appearance before a court, judge, or other judicial authority, whether
as a defendant, a witness, or a party to proceedings.

**Synonym:** **présentation devant le tribunal** (appearance before the court, a plain-language paraphrase); **présence
à l’audience** (presence at a hearing, close in some contexts but less precise)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la comparution**, plural **les comparutions**. Common combinations include **la
comparution devant le tribunal / devant le juge** (“appearance before the court / judge”), **une citation à
comparaître** (“a summons to appear”), **ordonner la comparution de quelqu’un** (“to order someone to appear”), and **la
comparution immédiate** (“an expedited criminal trial procedure”). The related verb is **comparaître**: **il comparaît –
il a comparu**. It is non-reflexive and intransitive, typically used with **devant**: **comparaître devant un tribunal**
(“to appear before a court”).

**Example:** *Le juge a ordonné la comparution du témoin.* — “The judge ordered the witness to appear.”

Another example: *Le prévenu sera jugé en comparution immédiate.* — “The defendant will be tried under an expedited
criminal procedure.”

**English:** **court appearance / appearance before a judge** · **German:** **Erscheinen vor Gericht**

Useful nuance: **Comparution** is a formal legal term, not the ordinary word for an appearance at an event or an
appearance in public. In France, **la comparution immédiate** names a specific procedure allowing certain criminal cases
to be brought rapidly before a criminal court; it does not simply mean any immediate court appearance. Do not confuse
**comparution** with **comparaison** (“comparison”).

## le renfort / en renfort

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Renfort** means extra help or support brought in to strengthen a team or deal with a difficult
situation. In the plural, **renforts** often refers to additional personnel, especially soldiers, police officers, or
emergency workers. It can also mean a piece or material used to strengthen a structure or object.

**Synonym:** **aide** (help, general); **soutien** (support); **appui** (backing or assistance); **consolidation**
(strengthening, in a structural context)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le renfort**, plural **les renforts**. Common combinations include **demander / appeler
des renforts** (“to request / call for reinforcements”), **envoyer des renforts** (“to send reinforcements”), **venir /
arriver en renfort** (“to come / arrive to help”), and **un renfort de personnel** (“additional staff”). **En renfort**
is a fixed phrase used without an article. In a technical context, **un renfort métallique** is “a metal reinforcement.”

**Example:** *Nous avons besoin de renfort pour terminer ce travail à temps.* — “We need extra help to finish this work
on time.”

Another example: *Deux collègues sont venus en renfort pendant la période de Noël.* — “Two colleagues came to help out
during the Christmas period.”

**English:** **extra help / reinforcements / reinforcement** · **German:** **Verstärkung / Unterstützung**

Useful nuance: **Renfort** usually implies additional help that supplements people or resources already available. **Du
renfort** presents this help as a general resource, while **des renforts** often highlights extra people or units being
sent in. The word is not limited to military situations: a busy shop or an overstretched team can also **demander du
renfort**. In construction or manufacturing, **un renfort** is a strengthening element. The expression **à grand renfort
de** means “with heavy use of” or “with a great deal of,” as in **à grand renfort de publicité** (“with a great deal of
advertising”).

## la faillite / faillites en chaîne

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Faillites en chaîne** means a series of business failures or bankruptcies occurring one after another,
often because the failure of one company causes others to fail. **Faillite** on its own means bankruptcy or,
figuratively, the complete failure of a policy, system, or undertaking.

**Synonym:** **faillites en cascade** (cascading bankruptcies); **une vague de faillites** (a wave of bankruptcies,
without necessarily implying that one causes another); **des défaillances d’entreprises** (business failures, a broader
economic term)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la faillite**, plural **les faillites**. The expression normally occurs in the plural:
**des faillites en chaîne**. **En chaîne** is invariable and means “in succession” or “in a chain reaction.” Common
combinations include **provoquer / entraîner des faillites en chaîne** (“to cause / lead to cascading bankruptcies”),
**une série de faillites en chaîne** (“a series of cascading bankruptcies”), and **faire faillite** (“to go bankrupt”).
In **faire faillite**, there is no article before **faillite**: **l’entreprise fait faillite**, **elle a fait
faillite**.

**Example:** *La fermeture de cette grande entreprise a provoqué des faillites en chaîne chez ses fournisseurs.* — “The
closure of this large company caused a chain of bankruptcies among its suppliers.”

Another example: *Les banques craignent des faillites en chaîne si la crise s’aggrave.* — “Banks fear cascading
bankruptcies if the crisis worsens.”

**English:** **cascading bankruptcies / a chain of business failures** · **German:** **eine Pleitewelle / eine Kette von
Unternehmensinsolvenzen**

Useful nuance: **En chaîne** often suggests a domino effect: one company fails, cannot pay its suppliers, and puts those
suppliers at risk of failing too. It can also describe failures occurring in rapid succession without establishing a
direct causal link. **Faillite** is common in everyday language and journalism; precise legal terms depend on the
jurisdiction and may include **cessation des paiements**, **redressement judiciaire**, or **liquidation judiciaire**,
which are not interchangeable. Figuratively, **la faillite d’un système** means “the failure of a system,” not financial
bankruptcy.

## le licenciement

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Licenciement** means the termination of an employee’s employment contract by the employer: a dismissal
or, especially for economic reasons, a layoff. **Licenciements** is the plural form.

**Synonym:** **renvoi** (dismissal, less formal and less legally precise); **congédiement** (dismissal, especially
common in Canadian French)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le licenciement**, plural **les licenciements**. Common combinations include **un
licenciement économique** (“a redundancy / layoff for economic reasons”), **un licenciement pour faute grave**
(“dismissal for serious misconduct”), **des licenciements collectifs** (“collective dismissals / mass layoffs”), **une
vague de licenciements** (“a wave of layoffs”), and **procéder à des licenciements** (“to carry out layoffs /
dismissals”). The related verb is **licencier quelqu’un** (“to dismiss / lay someone off”), a regular **-er** verb: **il
licencie**, **il a licencié**. The passive construction **être licencié(e)** means “to be dismissed / laid off.”

**Example:** *L’entreprise a annoncé une vague de licenciements.* — “The company announced a wave of layoffs.”

Another example: *Elle a contesté son licenciement devant le conseil de prud’hommes.* — “She challenged her dismissal
before the employment tribunal.”

**English:** **dismissal / layoff / redundancy** · **German:** **Entlassung / Kündigung durch den Arbeitgeber**

Useful nuance: **Licenciement** is initiated by the employer, unlike **démission** (“resignation”), which is initiated
by the employee. It does not necessarily imply misconduct: **un licenciement économique** results from economic
circumstances, whereas **un licenciement pour motif personnel** concerns reasons relating to the employee. English
“layoff” usually suggests an economic reason; “dismissal” is broader. **Suppression de postes** means “elimination of
positions” and does not always involve dismissing employees. Despite the shared word family, **licenciement** does not
mean obtaining a **licence** (“degree / licence”).

## remplir quelque chose / remplir quelque chose de quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **A2**.

**Definition:** **remplir** means **to fill**: to put something into a container or space until it is full, or to occupy
that space. It also commonly means **to fill in / fill out** a form or document. In more abstract uses, it means **to
fulfil** a duty or condition, or **to perform** a role.

**Synonym:** **emplir** (to fill; often more literary); **compléter** (to complete or fill in missing information);
**accomplir** (to fulfil or carry out a duty); **satisfaire à** (to meet a requirement or condition)

**Grammar:** Regular second-group **-ir** verb: **remplir – remplissant – rempli**. Present tense: **je remplis, tu
remplis, il/elle remplit, nous remplissons, vous remplissez, ils/elles remplissent**. The compound past uses **avoir**:
**j’ai rempli**. Common patterns are **remplir quelque chose** and **remplir quelque chose de quelque chose**: **remplir
une bouteille d’eau**. The reflexive **se remplir de** means “to fill up with”: **La salle se remplit de spectateurs**.
Useful combinations include **remplir un formulaire**, **remplir une condition**, **remplir son devoir**, and **remplir
un rôle**.

**Example:** *Je remplis la bouteille d’eau avant de partir.* — “I fill the bottle with water before leaving.”

Another example: *Vous devez remplir ce formulaire et le signer.* — “You must fill out this form and sign it.”

**English:** **to fill / to fill in / to fulfil** · **German:** **füllen / ausfüllen / erfüllen**

Useful nuance: **Remplir** is used both for physical contents and for information entered into a document: **remplir un
verre**, **remplir un formulaire**. **Remplir un verre d’eau** means to fill a glass with water; **remplir le verre à
moitié** means to fill it halfway, so the verb does not always imply filling something to the brim. In **remplir les
conditions**, it means meeting the requirements, not filling in a document. **Remplir quelqu’un de joie** means “to fill
someone with joy.” The opposite in the physical sense is **vider** (“to empty”).

## infléchir quelque chose / s’infléchir

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** **infléchir** means **to alter the direction of** or **to shift** something, especially a policy, a
position, or a trend. It often suggests a measured change rather than a complete reversal. Literally, it can mean **to
bend** or **to curve** something. **S’infléchir** means to change direction, bend, or undergo a shift.

**Synonym:** **modifier** (to modify; a broader term); **réorienter** (to redirect); **faire évoluer** (to bring about a
change in); **courber** (to bend or curve, in the literal sense)

**Grammar:** Regular second-group verb in **-ir**, conjugated like **finir**: **j’infléchis**, **nous infléchissons**,
**ils infléchissent**; past participle **infléchi**; passé composé **j’ai infléchi**. It takes a direct object:
**infléchir une politique**, **infléchir sa position**, **infléchir une tendance**. The pronominal form uses **être** in
compound tenses: **La tendance s’est infléchie** (“The trend shifted”). The related noun is **une inflexion** (“a change
in direction; a bend”).

**Example:** *Le gouvernement a infléchi sa politique sous la pression de l’opinion publique.* — “The government shifted
its policy under pressure from public opinion.”

Another example: *Après plusieurs mois de hausse, la courbe des prix s’est infléchie vers le bas.* — “After several
months of increases, the price curve turned downward.”

**English:** **to shift / to redirect / to bend** · **German:** **ändern / eine andere Richtung geben / biegen**

Useful nuance: **Infléchir** is especially common in formal writing, journalism, and political or economic discussion.
**Infléchir sa position** means to adjust one’s stance, often by making it less rigid, but not necessarily to abandon
it. The word does not inherently mean “to reduce”: with a trend or curve, the direction of the change comes from context
or an addition such as **vers le bas**. Unlike **fléchir**, which can mean “to weaken” or “to give way,” **infléchir**
focuses on a change of direction. In ordinary conversation, **modifier** or **changer** is often more natural.

## la procuration

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **procuration** means **power of attorney** or **authorization to act on someone’s behalf**: permission
given to another person to carry out a specific act or certain transactions in your name. It also refers to the document
granting this authority. Common contexts include banking, administrative procedures, and voting.

**Synonym:** **mandat** (authority given to someone to act on another person’s behalf; a broader legal term);
**autorisation** (authorization or permission; broader and less precise); **pouvoir** (authority to represent someone,
especially in legal or administrative contexts)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **la procuration**, **une procuration**; plural **les procurations**. Common patterns
include **donner procuration à quelqu’un pour faire quelque chose** (“to authorize someone to do something on your
behalf”), **avoir procuration pour agir au nom de quelqu’un** (“to have authority to act in someone’s name”), and
**signer une procuration** (“to sign a power of attorney”). In **donner procuration** and **voter par procuration**, the
noun normally has no article.

**Example:** *J’ai donné procuration à ma sœur pour gérer mon compte bancaire.* — “I gave my sister power of attorney to
manage my bank account.”

Another example: *Comme je serai à l’étranger, je vais voter par procuration.* — “Since I’ll be abroad, I’m going to
vote by proxy.”

**English:** **power of attorney / authorization; proxy** · **German:** **Vollmacht; Stimmrechtsvollmacht**

Useful nuance: A **procuration** can be limited to one task; it does not necessarily grant broad control over someone’s
affairs. In **voter par procuration**, another person casts your vote on your behalf. Figuratively, **par procuration**
means **vicariously**, as in **vivre une aventure par procuration** (“to experience an adventure through someone else”).
The person authorized to act is the **mandataire**; **procuration** refers to the authority or document, not normally to
that person.

## la subvention

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A **subvention** is financial assistance given by a public authority or another organization to support
a project, activity, institution, or business.

**Synonym:** **aide financière** (financial assistance); **financement** (funding; broader in meaning)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **une subvention**, plural **des subventions**. Common patterns include **une subvention à
une association** (“a grant to an association”), **une subvention pour un projet** (“a grant for a project”), and **une
subvention de l’État** (“a government grant”). Common verb combinations are **demander une subvention**, **obtenir une
subvention**, **accorder une subvention**, and **verser une subvention** (“to apply for, obtain, award, and pay a
grant”). The related verb is **subventionner** (“to subsidize”).

**Example:** *L’association a obtenu une subvention pour rénover ses locaux.* — “The association obtained a grant to
renovate its premises.”

Another example: *L’État accorde des subventions aux entreprises qui investissent dans les énergies renouvelables.* —
“The government awards subsidies to companies that invest in renewable energy.”

**English:** **grant / subsidy** · **German:** **die Subvention / der Zuschuss / die Förderung**

Useful nuance: English often uses **grant** for funding awarded to a particular project or organization, and **subsidy**
for support intended to reduce costs or sustain an economic activity. French **subvention** covers both. Unlike **un
prêt** (“a loan”), a **subvention** normally does not have to be repaid, provided its conditions are met.

## le / la locataire

**CEFR:** roughly **A2**.

**Definition:** A **locataire** is someone who rents a home, room, or other property from its owner, paying rent to use
it.

**Synonym:** **preneur / preneuse à bail** (tenant or leaseholder; formal/legal)

**Grammar:** Noun with the same form for masculine and feminine: **un locataire**, **une locataire**; plural: **les

**English:** **tenant / renter** · **German:** **der Mieter / die Mieterin**

Useful nuance: A **locataire** rents a property, whereas a **propriétaire** owns it. The owner who rents it out is the
**propriétaire bailleur**, often simply called the **propriétaire**. **Locataire** can also refer to someone renting
something other than a home, such as commercial premises or a vehicle; **loueur / loueuse** usually refers to the person
or business offering something for rent, not the customer.

## le riverain / la riveraine

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A **riverain** is someone who lives next to or near a particular place, especially a street, road,
construction site, or river. The plural **les riverains** commonly means the local residents directly affected by
traffic, noise, or nearby works.

**Synonym:** **habitant du quartier** (local resident); **voisin / voisine** (neighbor, especially of a particular
place)

**Grammar:** Countable noun: **le riverain**, **la riveraine**; plural **les riverains**, **les riveraines**. The
requested form **riverains** is masculine plural and can refer to an all-male or mixed group. It is also an adjective:
**un pays riverain**, **une commune riveraine**. A common pattern is **riverain de**, as in **les riverains de cette
rue** (“the residents of this street”).

**Example:** *Les riverains se plaignent du bruit causé par les travaux.* — “Local residents are complaining about the
noise caused by the construction work.”

Another example: *Cette rue est interdite à la circulation, sauf aux riverains.* — “This street is closed to traffic
except for residents.”

**English:** **local resident / nearby resident** · **German:** **der Anwohner / die Anwohnerin; der Anlieger / die
Anliegerin**

Useful nuance: Despite its connection with **rive** (“bank” or “shore”), **riverain** does not refer only to people
living beside water. In everyday news and public notices, **les riverains** often means residents living alongside a
road or near a source of disturbance. On traffic signs, **sauf riverains** means “except for residents.” As an
adjective, it can also describe places bordering water: **les pays riverains de la Méditerranée** (“the countries
bordering the Mediterranean”).
