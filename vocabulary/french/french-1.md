## le châtiment

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A punishment or penalty imposed on someone for wrongdoing, often with a formal, literary, or severe
tone.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: *le châtiment*; plural: *les châtiments*. Related verb: *châtier* (“to punish”),
which is relatively formal or literary.

**Example:** *Il craignait le châtiment qui l’attendait.* — “He feared the
punishment that awaited him.”

**English:** **punishment** · **German:** **die Strafe / die Züchtigung**

## le vol à l’étalage

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** The act of stealing goods from a shop or store by taking merchandise without paying for it.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun phrase: *le vol à l’étalage*. *Vol* means “theft,” while *à l’étalage* refers to goods displayed for
sale. A person committing it can be called *un voleur à l’étalage / une voleuse à l’étalage*.

**Example:** *Il a été
arrêté pour vol à l’étalage.* — “He was arrested for shoplifting.”

**English:** **shoplifting** · **German:** **der Ladendiebstahl**

## le faisceau

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A group of things gathered together or converging in the same direction. Depending on context, it can
refer to a beam of light, a bundle of objects, or figuratively a collection of clues or evidence pointing toward the
same conclusion.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: *le faisceau*; plural: *les faisceaux*. Common expressions include *un
faisceau de lumière* (“a beam of light”) and *un faisceau d’indices* (“a body / collection of evidence”).

**Example:**
*Les enquêteurs disposaient d’un faisceau d’indices contre le suspect.* — “The investigators had a body of evidence
against the suspect.”

**English:** **beam / bundle / body of evidence** · **German:** **der Strahl / das Bündel / die Indizienlage**

## le prévenu

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A person accused of an offense and brought before a criminal court for judgment. In French legal usage,
the term generally refers to someone prosecuted before a *tribunal correctionnel* or *tribunal de police*.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: *le prévenu*; feminine: *la prévenue*; plural: *les prévenus / les prévenues*.

**Example:** *Le prévenu
a reconnu les faits devant le tribunal.* — “The defendant admitted the facts before the court.”

**English:** **defendant / accused** · **German:** **der Angeklagte / der Beschuldigte**

## assourdissant / assourdissante

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** Extremely loud or intense to the point of being overwhelming or almost deafening. It can also be used
figuratively for something strikingly intense.

**Grammar:** Adjective: *assourdissant* (masculine), *assourdissante*
(feminine); plural: *assourdissants / assourdissantes*. It comes from *assourdir* (“to deafen”).

**Example:** *Le bruit
de la foule était assourdissant.* — “The noise of the crowd was deafening.”

**English:** **deafening** · **German:** **ohrenbetäubend**

## étayer quelque chose

**Definition:** To support, strengthen, or substantiate an argument, claim, explanation, or conclusion by providing
evidence, examples, facts, or reasoning. Literally, it can also mean to prop up or support a structure.

**Grammar:** Verb: *étayer – étayé – a étayé*. It usually takes a direct object: *étayer une thèse / un argument / une affirmation*.

**Example:** *L’avocat a étayé son argumentation avec plusieurs témoignages.* — “The lawyer supported his argument with
several testimonies.”

**English:** **support / substantiate** · **German:** **untermauern / stützen / belegen**

## le casier judiciaire

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** The official record of a person’s criminal convictions and certain judicial decisions. Depending on
context, it can refer to the criminal record itself or to the official document containing that information.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun phrase: *le casier judiciaire*; plural: *les casiers judiciaires*. Common expressions

## déclencher quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To trigger, set off, start, or cause something to begin, often
suddenly. It is commonly used for events, reactions, conflicts, alarms, mechanisms, or processes.

**Grammar:** Transitive verb: *déclencher – déclencha – a déclenché*; **déclencher quelque chose** takes a direct
object. *Déclenchés* is the masculine plural past participle, used either in a compound tense when agreement is
required or adjectivally: *des événements déclenchés par…* (“events triggered by…”). Feminine plural: *déclenchées*.

**Example:** *Les incidents ont été déclenchés par une série de malentendus.* — “The incidents were triggered by a
series of misunderstandings.”

**English:** **trigger / set off / cause** · **German:** **auslösen / hervorrufen**

## dévoiler quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To reveal, uncover, disclose, or make something
previously hidden or unknown known.

**Grammar:** Transitive verb: *dévoiler – dévoila – a dévoilé*; **dévoiler quelque chose** takes a direct object.
*Dévoilant* is the present participle and can also
function in a gerund with *en*: *en dévoilant* (“by revealing / while revealing”).

**Example:** *Il poursuivit son
récit, dévoilant peu à peu la vérité.* — “He continued his story, gradually revealing the truth.”

**English:** **reveal / unveil / disclose** · **German:** **enthüllen / aufdecken / offenlegen**

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: *la
clémence*. Common expressions include *faire preuve de clémence* (“to show leniency / mercy”) and *demander la clémence

**Example:** *L’avocat a demandé au juge de faire preuve de clémence

**English:** **leniency / mercy / clemency** · **German:** **die Milde / die Nachsicht / die Gnade**

## entrevoir quelqu’un/quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To glimpse, catch sight of briefly, or perceive only partially.

**Example:** *Il avait entrevu une silhouette derrière la porte avant qu’elle ne disparaisse.* — “He had

**English:** **glimpse / catch sight of / dimly perceive** · **German:** **flüchtig sehen / erblicken / erahnen**

## le champ / le champ d’application

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** *Champ* can mean a field, area, or domain. In the expression *champ d’application*, it refers to the

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: *le champ*;

**English:** **field / scope; scope of application** · **German:** **das Feld / der Bereich; der Anwendungsbereich**

## en vertu de quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A formal expression meaning that something is done **on the basis of**, **by authority of**, or **in
accordance with** a law, rule, agreement, right, or principle.

**Grammar:** Prepositional phrase: *en vertu de + noun*.
It is especially common in legal, administrative, and formal contexts.

**Example:** *Il a agi en vertu de la loi.* — “He
acted under the authority of the law.”

**English:** **under / pursuant to / by virtue of** · **German:** **kraft / aufgrund / gemäß**

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Grammar:** Adjectival phrase, usually used after a noun or with *être*: *une loi en

force / take effect”).

**Example:** *Cette loi est en vigueur depuis janvier.* — “This law has been in force since

**English:** **in force / in effect / currently applicable** · **German:** **in Kraft / geltend / gültig**

## le cas échéant

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A formal expression meaning **if applicable**, **where appropriate**, or **if necessary**, depending on
the context. It indicates that something should be done only if the relevant situation arises.

**Grammar:** Adverbial
expression. It is especially common in legal, administrative, and formal writing.

**Example:** *Le demandeur doit
fournir, le cas échéant, les documents justificatifs.* — “The applicant must provide supporting documents, if
applicable.”

**English:** **if applicable / where appropriate / if necessary** · **German:** **gegebenenfalls / falls nötig**

## incomber à quelqu’un

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** To be the responsibility, duty, or obligation of someone.
It is often used in formal, legal, or administrative contexts.

**Grammar:** Non-reflexive verb: *incomber – incomba – a incombé*. In **incomber à quelqu’un**, the duty is the subject
and the responsible person is an indirect object introduced by **à**: *Cette tâche m’incombe*. *Incombant* is the
present participle. A common structure is *les obligations
incombant à quelqu’un* (“the obligations incumbent upon someone”).

**Example:** *Les obligations incombant aux parties
doivent être respectées.* — “The obligations incumbent upon the parties must be respected.”

**English:** **be incumbent upon / fall to / be the responsibility of** · **German:** **obliegen / zufallen**

## la dispense

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** An exemption or waiver that releases someone from an obligation, requirement, duty, or rule that would
normally apply.

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: *la dispense*; plural: *les dispenses*. Common expressions include *une
dispense de quelque chose* (“an exemption from something”) and *accorder une dispense* (“to grant an exemption”).
Related verb: *dispenser quelqu’un de quelque chose* (“to exempt someone from something”).

**Example:** *Le tribunal

**English:** **exemption / waiver / dispensation** · **German:** **die Befreiung / die Freistellung**

## ainsi / ainsi que

**CEFR:** roughly **B1–B2**; formal uses are often **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** An adverb meaning **thus**, **in
this way**, or **therefore**. It can also introduce a consequence or summarize what has just been said. In the fixed
conjunction **ainsi que**, it means **as well as**, **and**, or **just as**, depending on the sentence.

**Synonym:** **de cette manière / donc / de même** depending on context

**Grammar:** Invariable adverb. **Ainsi** commonly modifies a verb: *Il a ainsi réglé le problème* (“He solved the problem in this
way”). At the beginning of a sentence, **Ainsi,** can mean “therefore” or “so”: *Ainsi, nous avons décidé de partir*.
**Ainsi que + noun/pronoun** adds another element: *Elle parle français ainsi qu’anglais* (“She speaks French as well as
English”). Before a clause, **ainsi que** can mean “just as”: *Ainsi qu’il l’avait annoncé, il est venu à l’heure*.
Formal or fixed expressions include **ainsi soit-il** (“so be it”), **ainsi va la vie** (“such is life”), and **ainsi
nommé** (“so-called / thus named”).

**Example:** *Il a expliqué la règle, ainsi que les exceptions.* — “He explained the
rule as well as the exceptions.”

Another example: *Ainsi, chacun peut comprendre la décision.* — “Thus, everyone can understand the decision.”

**English:** **thus / so / in this way / therefore**; compound conjunction: **as well as / and / just as** · **German:**
**so / auf diese Weise / folglich**; compound conjunction: **sowie / und / ebenso wie**

Useful nuance: **Ainsi** is more formal than everyday **comme ça** (“like that / this way”) and often appears in written
or carefully structured speech. **Ainsi que** can coordinate nouns, but when it introduces a comparison or subordinate
clause, it is closer to **just as** than to a simple “and.”

## se pencher / se pencher sur quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B1–B2**.

**Definition:** To bend or lean the upper body,
usually toward something or someone; figuratively, **se pencher sur quelque chose** means to examine, study, or give
careful attention to a subject or problem.

**Synonym:** **s’incliner / se courber / examiner** depending on context

**Grammar:** Regular pronominal **-er** verb: **se pencher – se pencha –
s’est penché**. **Se pencha** is the third-person singular **passé simple** form (“he/she leaned or bent”). Use **se
pencher sur + noun** to mean “to look into or examine something”: *se pencher sur un problème*. Use **se pencher vers +
noun/person** for movement toward something: *se pencher vers la fenêtre*. Use **se pencher au-dessus de + noun** for
leaning over something: *se pencher au-dessus du berceau*. The past participle agrees with the subject in the usual
pronominal construction: *elle s’est penchée*.

**Example:** *Il se pencha sur la table pour lire le document.* — “He
leaned over the table to read the document.”

Another example: *La commission se penchera sur cette question la semaine prochaine.* — “The commission will examine
this issue next week.”

**English:** **lean over / bend toward / look into / examine** · **German:** **sich beugen / sich
über etwas beugen / sich mit etwas befassen**

Useful nuance: The physical meaning describes a change in posture, while **se pencher sur** is usually figurative and
means to study or address a topic. Do not confuse **pencher** with **penser** (“to think”); *se pencher sur un problème*
means “to look into a problem.”

## redresser quelque chose / se redresser

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To straighten something that is bent or out of position; figuratively, to correct a situation,
repair a decline, restore finances, or remedy an injustice. Reflexively, **se redresser** means to straighten up, sit or
stand upright, or recover after a setback.

**Synonym:** **remettre droit / corriger / rétablir**; **se redresser:** **se relever / se tenir droit** depending on
context

**Grammar:** Regular **-er** verb: **redresser – redressait – a redressé**. **Redressait** is the third-person singular
**imparfait** form (“he/she was straightening, correcting, or restoring”).
It usually takes a direct object: **redresser quelque chose (direct object)**, as in *redresser la tête*, *redresser une
situation*, or *redresser les finances*. The pronominal verb is **se redresser – se redressait – s’est redressé**;
common expressions include **se redresser après quelque chose** and **se redresser à la suite de quelque chose**. In
**redresser un tort**, *tort* is the direct object and the phrase means “to remedy a wrong.”

**Example:** *Elle se
redressa lentement après sa chute.* — “She slowly straightened up after her fall.”

Another example: *Le gouvernement cherche à redresser les finances publiques.* — “The government is seeking to restore
the public finances.”

**English:** **straighten / put right / correct / restore**; reflexive: **straighten up / recover** · **German:**
**aufrichten / korrigieren / wiederherstellen**; **sich aufrichten / sich erholen**

Useful nuance: **Redresser** often implies correcting something that has gone wrong or restoring it to a better state.
**Rétablir** emphasizes restoring a previous condition, while **corriger** focuses on removing an error. The figurative
noun **un redressement** can mean a recovery or turnaround.

## le sursaut

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A sudden involuntary movement or start, usually caused by surprise, fear, a loud noise, or a sudden
sensation. Figuratively, it can mean a sudden renewed effort, reaction, or recovery after a period of weakness or
inactivity.

**Synonym:** **le bond / le tressaillement / le réveil soudain**; figuratively **le regain / la réaction**

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le sursaut**; plural: **les sursauts**. Common constructions include **faire
un sursaut**, **avoir un sursaut de surprise**, **sursauter de peur**, and **faire sursauter quelqu’un** (“to make
someone start”). The related intransitive verb is **sursauter – sursauta – a sursauté**. In figurative use, **un sursaut
de + noun** introduces a renewed burst: *un sursaut d’énergie*, *un sursaut de croissance*.

**Example:** *Le bruit
soudain lui a fait faire un sursaut.* — “The sudden noise made him start.”

Another example: *L’économie connaît un sursaut après plusieurs années difficiles.* — “The economy is experiencing a
rebound after several difficult years.”

**English:** **start / jolt / sudden movement**; figuratively **upsurge /
recovery / renewed effort** · **German:** **der Schreck / das Zusammenzucken / der Aufschwung / das Aufbäumen**

Useful nuance: **Sursaut** describes a brief, sudden reaction or a renewed burst of energy. **Bond** emphasizes a jump,
while **tressaillement** focuses on an involuntary twitch or shiver. In figurative contexts, **un sursaut** often
suggests a last or unexpected recovery.

## amorcer quelque chose / s’amorcer

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To begin or
initiate a process, action, conversation, movement, or change. It can also mean to prime or start a mechanism, bait a
hook or trap, or begin a turn or maneuver. Reflexively, **s’amorcer** means to begin or start taking shape.

**Synonym:** **commencer / entamer / mettre en route / engager** depending on context

**Grammar:** Regular **-er** verb with a spelling change: **amorcer – nous amorçons – amorça – a amorcé**. The cedilla in **nous
amorçons** keeps the **c** soft before **-ons**. It usually takes a direct object: **amorcer quelque chose (direct object)**, for example *amorcer une discussion*, *amorcer un virage*, *amorcer une pompe*, or *amorcer un
changement*. The pronominal form is **s’amorcer – s’amorçait – s’est amorcé**: *Le processus s’amorce progressivement*
(“The process is gradually beginning”).

**Example:** *Le ministre a amorcé une discussion avec les représentants
syndicaux.* — “The minister began a discussion with the union representatives.”

Another example: *La voiture a amorcé un virage à droite.* — “The car began turning to the right.”

**English:** **begin / initiate / start / set in motion / prime** · **German:** **beginnen / einleiten / in Gang setzen /
vorbereiten**

Useful nuance: **Amorcer** is more formal and deliberate than **commencer** and often suggests the first stage of a
process. **Entamer** also means “begin,” especially for a discussion, task, or meal, while **amorcer** emphasizes
setting something in motion.

## parvenir à quelque chose / parvenir à faire quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To succeed in doing something or
achieve a result, often after difficulty or sustained effort. It can also mean to reach a place, person, or destination,
or for information or an object to reach someone.

**Synonym:** **réussir à / arriver à / atteindre** depending on context

**Grammar:** Irregular verb: **parvenir – parvint – est parvenu**. **Parvint** is the third-person singular **passé
simple** form (“he/she succeeded or reached”). Use **parvenir à +
infinitive**: *parvenir à résoudre un problème* (“succeed in solving a problem”), or **parvenir à + noun** for reaching
or attaining something: *parvenir à un accord* (“reach an agreement”). For a destination, use **parvenir jusqu’à +
place/person**: *parvenir jusqu’au sommet*. Information can **parvenir à quelqu’un** (“reach someone”), with **à +
indirect object**.

**Example:** *Après plusieurs tentatives, elle parvint à ouvrir la porte.* — “After several attempts,
she managed to open the door.”

Another example: *Le message ne lui est jamais parvenu.* — “The message never reached him.”

**English:** **succeed in /
manage to / reach / attain** · **German:** **es schaffen / gelingen / erreichen / gelangen**

Useful nuance: **Parvenir à** is more formal than everyday **réussir à** or **arriver à** and often emphasizes effort or
difficulty. In the passé composé it takes **être**: *elle est parvenue à ses fins* (“she achieved her aims”).

## cerner quelqu’un/quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** To surround or encircle
a person, place, or object; figuratively, to identify the limits or essential nature of a problem, situation, or idea
and understand it clearly.

**Synonym:** **encercler / entourer / délimiter / saisir** depending on context

**Grammar:** Regular **-er** verb: **cerner – cerna – a cerné**. **Cerné** is the masculine
singular past participle; feminine and plural forms are **cernée, cernés, cernées**. It takes a direct object: **cerner
quelqu’un/quelque chose (direct object)**. In the passive or adjectival construction, use **être cerné par + noun** for
what surrounds someone or something: *être cerné par l’ennemi*. Common figurative patterns include **cerner un
problème**, **cerner les causes**, and **cerner la personnalité de quelqu’un**.

**Example:** *Les soldats étaient cernés
par l’ennemi.* — “The soldiers were surrounded by the enemy.”

Another example: *Les chercheurs essaient de cerner les causes du phénomène.* — “The researchers are trying to identify
the causes of the phenomenon.”

**English:** **surround / encircle / identify / grasp** · **German:** **umzingeln /
einkreisen / erfassen / erkennen**

Useful nuance: In the physical sense, **cerner** is close to **encercler**, but it can sound more formal. In the
figurative sense, **cerner** means to understand or define something precisely, not merely to notice it.
