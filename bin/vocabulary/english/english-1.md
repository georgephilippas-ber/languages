CEFR levels are approximate learning estimates, not official classifications of individual words. Nouns use the singular
headword; verb headings use to + infinitive and show their object or preposition patterns. Adjectives retain their
adjective form, including participial adjectives.

## sequinned

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** Covered or decorated with small, shiny discs called sequins, especially on clothing or accessories.

**Synonym:** **sparkly / decorated with sequins / glittering**

**Grammar:** Adjective; from *sequin*. Common combinations include *sequinned dress*, *sequinned jacket*, and *sequinned
top*.

**Example:** *She wore a sequinned dress that caught the light as she moved.*

**German:** **mit Pailletten besetzt / paillettenbesetzt** · **French:** **pailleté / à sequins**

## distended

**CEFR:** roughly **C1–C2**.

**Definition:** Abnormally expanded, stretched, or swollen, especially because of internal pressure, gas, fluid, or
accumulated contents. It is often used in medical or anatomical contexts.

**Synonym:** **swollen / enlarged / bloated**

**Grammar:** Adjective; past participle of *distend*. Common combinations include *distended abdomen*, *distended
veins*, and *distended stomach*.

**Example:** *The patient complained of pain and a visibly distended abdomen.*

**German:** **aufgebläht / geschwollen / erweitert** · **French:** **distendu / gonflé / dilaté**

## unpropitious

**CEFR:** roughly **C2**.

**Definition:** Unfavorable or unlikely to lead to success; not presenting good conditions or circumstances for
something to happen. It can also describe a moment, situation, or environment that is particularly unsuitable or
disadvantageous.

**Grammar:** Adjective: *unpropitious*. It is the opposite of *propitious* (“favorable, likely to produce a good
result”). Common constructions include *an unpropitious time*, *unpropitious circumstances*, and *conditions
unpropitious for/to something*. The word is **formal and somewhat literary**.

**Example:** *They began their journey under unpropitious circumstances.* — « Ils ont commencé leur voyage dans des
circonstances défavorables. »

**German:** **ungünstig / unvorteilhaft / unheilverkündend** · **French:** **défavorable / peu propice / de mauvais
augure**

## to brook something

**CEFR:** roughly **C2**.

**Definition:** To tolerate, allow, or put up with something, especially opposition, delay, interference, or
contradiction. It is most commonly encountered in the negative construction *to brook no...*, meaning “to tolerate
no...” or “to allow no...”.

**Grammar:** Regular transitive verb: *brook – brooked – brooked*. **To brook something** takes a direct object and is
not reflexive. *Brooking* is the present participle and gerund. Common constructions include *brook no opposition*,
*brook no delay*, *brook no interference*, and *brook no contradiction*. The verb is literary or formal and is uncommon
in everyday conversation.

**Example:** *She was in no mood for brooking any further delay.* — « Elle n’était pas disposée à tolérer le moindre
retard supplémentaire. »

**German:** **etwas dulden / hinnehmen / zulassen** · **French:** **tolérer / supporter / admettre**

## inscrutable

**CEFR:** roughly **C1–C2**.

**Definition:** Very difficult or impossible to understand, interpret, or explain, especially when referring to a
person’s expression, motives, behavior, or something complex and obscure.

**Synonym:** **mysterious / enigmatic / unfathomable**

**Grammar:** Adjective. Common combinations include *inscrutable expression*, *inscrutable motives*, and *inscrutable
smile*.

**Example:** *He listened in silence, his expression completely inscrutable.*

**German:** **unergründlich / undurchschaubar / rätselhaft** · **French:** **impénétrable / énigmatique / insondable**

## tracksuit

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** A matching set of casual or sports clothing consisting of a jacket or sweatshirt and trousers,
originally designed to be worn before, during, or after athletic activity. Tracksuits are also commonly worn as everyday
casual clothing.

**Grammar:** Countable noun: *a tracksuit*; plural: *tracksuits*. Common constructions include *wear a tracksuit*, *a
tracksuit top*, and *tracksuit bottoms*. In American English, *sweatsuit* may sometimes be used for a similar type of
clothing.

**Example:** *He turned up to the meeting wearing a tracksuit and trainers.* — « Il est arrivé à la réunion en
survêtement et en baskets. »

**German:** **der Trainingsanzug / der Jogginganzug** · **French:** **survêtement**

## desultory

**CEFR:** roughly **C2**.

**Definition:** Lacking a clear plan, purpose, or sustained effort; moving irregularly from one thing to another without
much consistency or direction. It can describe conversation, work, reading, attempts, or activity.

**Synonym:** **aimless / unfocused / haphazard**

**Grammar:** Adjective. Common combinations include *desultory conversation*, *desultory efforts*, *desultory reading*,
and *desultory progress*.

**Example:** *After a few desultory attempts to solve the problem, he gave up.*

**German:** **planlos / sprunghaft / halbherzig** · **French:** **décousu / désordonné / sans suite**

## to dawdle / to dawdle over something

**CEFR:** roughly **C1**.

**Definition:** To move, act, or complete something more slowly than necessary, often because of distraction,
reluctance, or lack of urgency.

**Synonym:** **linger / procrastinate / waste time**

**Grammar:** Verb: *dawdle – dawdled – dawdled*. Common constructions include *dawdle over something* and *dawdle
along*.

**Example:** *Stop dawdling or we’ll miss the train.*

**German:** **trödeln / bummeln / Zeit vertrödeln** · **French:** **traîner / lambiner / perdre son temps**
