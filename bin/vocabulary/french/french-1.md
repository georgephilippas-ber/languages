# French Vocabulary

CEFR levels are approximate learning estimates, not official classifications of individual words. Nouns have definite
articles; verbs use the present infinitive, with se/s’ for pronominal forms. Complements show direct objects or the
required prepositions; modern French nouns do not inflect for accusative or dative case. Inflected source forms remain
in the grammar notes.

## la haine

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** A very strong feeling of hostility, aversion, or hatred toward a person, group, idea, or thing.

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: *la haine*. It is usually singular, though *les haines* can appear in literary or formal
contexts. Common expressions include *éprouver de la haine pour quelqu’un* (“to feel hatred toward someone”) and
*attiser la haine* (“to fuel hatred”).

**Example:** *Il parlait avec une haine profonde de ceux qui l’avaient trahi.* —
“He spoke with deep hatred of those who had betrayed him.”

**English:** **hatred / hate** · **German:** **der Hass**

## le licenciement

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Licenciement** means the termination of an employee’s employment contract by the employer: a dismissal
or, especially for economic reasons, a layoff. **Licenciements** is the plural form.

**Synonym:** **renvoi** (dismissal, less formal and less legally precise); **congédiement** (dismissal, especially
common in Canadian French)

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le licenciement**, plural **les licenciements**. Common combinations include **un
licenciement économique** (“a redundancy / layoff for economic reasons”), **un licenciement pour faute grave**
(“dismissal for serious misconduct”), **des licenciements collectifs** (“collective dismissals / mass layoffs”), **une
vague de licenciements** (“a wave of layoffs”), and **procéder à des licenciements** (“to carry out layoffs /
dismissals”). The related verb is **licencier quelqu’un** (“to dismiss / lay someone off”), a regular **-er** verb: **il
licencie**, **il a licencié**. The passive construction **être licencié(e)** means “to be dismissed / laid off.”

**Example:** *L’entreprise a annoncé une vague de licenciements.* — “The company announced a wave of layoffs.”

Another example: *Elle a contesté son licenciement devant le conseil de prud’hommes.* — “She challenged her dismissal
before the employment tribunal.”

**English:** **dismissal / layoff / redundancy** · **German:** **Entlassung / Kündigung durch den Arbeitgeber**

Useful nuance: **Licenciement** is initiated by the employer, unlike **démission** (“resignation”), which is initiated
by the employee. It does not necessarily imply misconduct: **un licenciement économique** results from economic
circumstances, whereas **un licenciement pour motif personnel** concerns reasons relating to the employee. English
“layoff” usually suggests an economic reason; “dismissal” is broader. **Suppression de postes** means “elimination of
positions” and does not always involve dismissing employees. Despite the shared word family, **licenciement** does not
mean obtaining a **licence** (“degree / licence”).

## le gage de justice

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** Something that serves as a guarantee, sign, or assurance that justice or fairness will be respected.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun phrase: *un gage de justice*. The construction *un gage de quelque chose* means “a
guarantee / token / sign of something.”

**Example:** *La transparence est un gage de justice.* — “Transparency is a
guarantee of fairness.”

**English:** **a guarantee of fairness / a sign of justice** · **German:** **eine Garantie für Gerechtigkeit / ein
Zeichen der Fairness**

## en vertu de quelque chose

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A formal expression meaning that something is done **on the basis of**, **by authority of**, or **in
accordance with** a law, rule, agreement, right, or principle.

**Grammar:** Prepositional phrase: *en vertu de + noun*.
It is especially common in legal, administrative, and formal contexts.

**Example:** *Il a agi en vertu de la loi.* — “He
acted under the authority of the law.”

**English:** **under / pursuant to / by virtue of** · **German:** **kraft / aufgrund / gemäß**

## le sursaut

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** A sudden involuntary movement or start, usually caused by surprise, fear, a loud noise, or a sudden
sensation. Figuratively, it can mean a sudden renewed effort, reaction, or recovery after a period of weakness or
inactivity.

**Synonym:** **le bond / le tressaillement / le réveil soudain**; figuratively **le regain / la réaction**

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: **le sursaut**; plural: **les sursauts**. Common constructions include **faire
un sursaut**, **avoir un sursaut de surprise**, **sursauter de peur**, and **faire sursauter quelqu’un** (“to make
someone start”). The related intransitive verb is **sursauter – sursauta – a sursauté**. In figurative use, **un sursaut
de + noun** introduces a renewed burst: *un sursaut d’énergie*, *un sursaut de croissance*.

**Example:** *Le bruit
soudain lui a fait faire un sursaut.* — “The sudden noise made him start.”

Another example: *L’économie connaît un sursaut après plusieurs années difficiles.* — “The economy is experiencing a
rebound after several difficult years.”

**English:** **start / jolt / sudden movement**; figuratively **upsurge /
recovery / renewed effort** · **German:** **der Schreck / das Zusammenzucken / der Aufschwung / das Aufbäumen**

Useful nuance: **Sursaut** describes a brief, sudden reaction or a renewed burst of energy. **Bond** emphasizes a jump,
while **tressaillement** focuses on an involuntary twitch or shiver. In figurative contexts, **un sursaut** often
suggests a last or unexpected recovery.

## pâle

**CEFR:** roughly **A2–B1**.

**Definition:** Light in color, especially with little or
no healthy color in a person’s face; it can also describe a weak, faint, or washed-out color, light, or impression.

**Synonym:** **blême / décoloré / clair** depending on context

**Grammar:** Adjective with the same spelling in the masculine and feminine: **pâle**; plural: **pâles**. It agrees in
number, but not in gender: *un visage pâle*, *une peau pâle*, *des couleurs pâles*. Common constructions include **être
pâle**, **devenir pâle**, **pâle de peur/de colère** (*de + noun* explains the cause), and **pâle comme un linge** (“as
pale as a sheet”).

**Example:** *Elle est devenue pâle de peur en entendant le bruit.* — “She went pale with fear when
she heard the noise.”

Another example: *La pièce était éclairée par une lumière pâle.* — “The room was lit by a pale light.”

**English:** **pale / pallid / wan / faint** · **German:** **blass / blassfarben / hell**

Useful nuance: **Pâle** is the general word for a lack of color or a light, weak shade. **Blême** is stronger and often
suggests illness, shock, or fear; **clair** simply means light in color and does not necessarily imply that a person
looks unhealthy.

## mettre quelque chose à dériver

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**; the nautical use is specialized.

**Definition:** To set or
place something so that it drifts, usually on water or in the air, without being steered or controlled. Figuratively, it
can mean to allow a situation or discussion to drift without direction.

**Synonym:** **laisser dériver / faire dériver / abandonner au courant** depending on context

**Grammar:** The construction is **mettre
quelque chose (direct object) à dériver**: *mettre un bateau à dériver*. **Mettre** is an irregular verb: **mettre – mis –
a mis**. **Mis à dériver** is the masculine singular past participle phrase; feminine and plural forms agree with the
noun: *mise à dériver*, *mis à dériver*, *mises à dériver*. **Dériver** is an intransitive **-er** verb: **dériver –
dériva – a dérivé** (“to drift”). In the passive, use **être mis à dériver**: *Le radeau a été mis à dériver*.

**Example:** *Les marins ont mis le canot à dériver après avoir coupé le moteur.* — “The sailors set the boat adrift
after switching off the engine.”

Another example: *Le radeau, mis à dériver par la tempête, s’éloigna rapidement du rivage.* — “The raft, set adrift by
the storm, quickly moved away from the shore.”

**English:** **set adrift / let drift / allow to drift** · **German:** **etwas treiben lassen / etwas abtreiben lassen**

Useful nuance: **Dériver** by itself means “to drift” or, figuratively, “to go off course.” **Mettre à dériver** is
causative: someone or something causes the object to begin drifting. The accent in **à** is required.

## le prévenu

**CEFR:** roughly **B2–C1**.

**Definition:** A person accused of an offense and brought before a criminal court for judgment. In French legal usage,
the term generally refers to someone prosecuted before a *tribunal correctionnel* or *tribunal de police*.

**Grammar:** Masculine noun: *le prévenu*; feminine: *la prévenue*; plural: *les prévenus / les prévenues*.

**Example:** *Le prévenu
a reconnu les faits devant le tribunal.* — “The defendant admitted the facts before the court.”

**English:** **defendant / accused** · **German:** **der Angeklagte / der Beschuldigte**
