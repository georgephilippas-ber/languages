## etwas (Akkusativ) rechtfertigen / sich (Akkusativ) rechtfertigen

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Rechtfertigen** means **to justify** something: to give reasons showing that an action, decision, or
claim is reasonable, acceptable, or necessary. It can also mean that something provides sufficient grounds for an action
or judgment, as in **Die Ergebnisse rechtfertigen den Aufwand** (“The results justify the effort”). Reflexive **sich
rechtfertigen** means **to justify oneself** or **to defend one’s actions**, especially in response to criticism or
accusations.

**Synonym:** **begründen** (to give reasons for something; more neutral and not necessarily defensive); **verteidigen**
(to defend an action, position, or person against criticism); **legitimieren** (to legitimize; more formal, often
involving authority or accepted standards)

**Grammar:** Regular verb: **rechtfertigt – rechtfertigte – hat gerechtfertigt**. It is not separable; the past
participle is **gerechtfertigt**, and the infinitive with **zu** is **zu rechtfertigen**. Common patterns include
**etwas (Akkusativ) rechtfertigen** (“to justify something”), **etwas mit etwas (Dativ) rechtfertigen** (“to justify
something by citing something”), **sich (Akkusativ) für etwas (Akkusativ) rechtfertigen** (“to justify oneself for
something”), and **sich vor jemandem (Dativ) rechtfertigen** (“to justify oneself to someone”). The reflexive pronoun
changes with the subject: **ich rechtfertige mich**, **du rechtfertigst dich**, **sie rechtfertigt sich**.

**Example:** *Die Firma rechtfertigte die Preiserhöhung mit gestiegenen Energiekosten.* — “The company justified the
price increase by citing higher energy costs.”

Another example: *Du musst dich nicht für jede Entscheidung rechtfertigen.* — “You don’t have to justify yourself for
every decision.”

**English:** **to justify / to justify oneself** · **French:** **justifier / se justifier**

Useful nuance: **Rechtfertigen** goes beyond simply explaining why something happened: it presents something as
justified or defensible. **Begründen** can neutrally explain the reasons for a decision, while **sich rechtfertigen**
often suggests that someone feels challenged or accused. Justifying an action does not necessarily mean apologizing for
it; **sich entschuldigen** means “to apologize.” The phrase **Das rechtfertigt nicht …** (“That does not justify …”)
rejects a reason as insufficient. **Gerechtfertigt** is also commonly used as an adjective meaning “justified” or
“warranted,” as in **gerechtfertigte Kritik** (“justified criticism”). The related noun is **die Rechtfertigung**
(“justification”).

## das Gebiet

**CEFR:** roughly **B1**.

**Definition:** **Gebiet** means an **area**, **region**, or **territory**: a part of a country, landscape, or other
geographical space. It also means a **field** or **area of activity or knowledge**, such as medicine, research, or
technology.

**Synonym:** **Region** (region; a geographical area, often with shared characteristics); **Gegend** (area or locality;
often used when describing surroundings); **Territorium** (territory; especially an area under political control);
**Bereich** (area or field; especially a sphere of activity or knowledge)

**Grammar:** Neuter noun: **das Gebiet**, genitive **des Gebiets / des Gebietes**, plural **die Gebiete**. Common
patterns include **in einem Gebiet (Dativ)** (“in an area”), **in ein Gebiet (Akkusativ)** (“into an area”), and **auf
dem Gebiet der Medizin / Technik** (“in the field of medicine / technology”). For expertise, use **auf einem Gebiet
(Dativ)**, as in **auf diesem Gebiet erfahren sein** (“to be experienced in this field”).

**Example:** *Nach dem starken Regen wurden mehrere Gebiete überschwemmt.* — “Several areas were flooded after the heavy
rain.”

Another example: *Sie ist Expertin auf dem Gebiet der erneuerbaren Energien.* — “She is an expert in the field of
renewable energy.”

**English:** **area / region / territory / field** · **French:** **zone / région / territoire / domaine**

Useful nuance: **Gebiet** often presents an area as a distinct geographical unit, without necessarily implying official
borders. **Gegend** is more typical for someone's surroundings or a loosely defined locality, while **Territorium**
emphasizes political control or possession. In the figurative sense, **auf diesem Gebiet** means “in this field,”
whereas **in diesem Gebiet** usually refers to a geographical location. Common compounds include **das Wohngebiet**
(“residential area”), **das Naturschutzgebiet** (“nature reserve”), and **das Fachgebiet** (“specialist field”).

## die Eitelkeit

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **Eitelkeit** means **vanity**: excessive pride in one’s appearance, abilities, or importance, often
combined with a desire to be admired. It can concern physical attractiveness, but also intelligence, professional
success, or social status. In older, literary, or religious language, it can also mean **futility** or **the transience
of worldly things**.

**Synonym:** **Selbstgefälligkeit** (self-satisfaction or smugness; being overly pleased with oneself);
**Geltungsdrang** (a strong desire for recognition or attention); **Gefallsucht** (an excessive desire to please or be
admired; strongly disapproving)

**Grammar:** Feminine noun: **die Eitelkeit**, genitive **der Eitelkeit**, plural **die Eitelkeiten**. Usually
uncountable when referring to the character trait; the plural can refer to individual displays of vanity or personal
sensitivities connected with pride. Common patterns include **aus Eitelkeit** (“out of vanity”), **jemandes Eitelkeit
verletzen** (“to wound someone’s vanity”), and **jemandes Eitelkeit schmeicheln (Dativ)** (“to flatter someone’s
vanity”), as in **Das Lob schmeichelt seiner Eitelkeit**. The related adjective is **eitel** (“vain”).

**Example:** *Aus Eitelkeit wollte er nicht zugeben, dass er einen Fehler gemacht hatte.* — “Out of vanity, he did not
want to admit that he had made a mistake.”

Another example: *Das Kompliment schmeichelte ihrer Eitelkeit.* — “The compliment flattered her vanity.”

**English:** **vanity** · **French:** **vanité**

Useful nuance: **Eitelkeit** usually has a critical or disapproving tone, though it can also be used lightly or
humorously. It is not limited to concern about appearance: **intellektuelle Eitelkeit** means “intellectual vanity.”
Unlike **Stolz** (“pride”), which can be justified and positive, **Eitelkeit** suggests excessive self-regard or a need
for admiration. **Persönliche Eitelkeiten** often refers to wounded pride or self-important concerns that interfere with
cooperation. The older sense of “futility” or “transience” occurs especially in literary and religious contexts, as in
**die Eitelkeit der Welt** (“the vanity of the world”).

## unverzeihlich

**CEFR:** roughly **B2**.

**Definition:** **unverzeihlich** means **unforgivable**: so wrong, hurtful, or serious that it cannot or should not be
forgiven. It can also mean **inexcusable**, especially when describing a mistake, omission, or failure that should
clearly have been avoided.

**Synonym:** **unverzeihbar** (unforgivable; very close in meaning); **unentschuldbar** (inexcusable; emphasizing that
no excuse can justify something); **nicht zu verzeihen** (not to be forgiven; a common equivalent phrase)

**Grammar:** Adjective, used attributively, predicatively, and adverbially: **ein unverzeihlicher Fehler** (“an
unforgivable mistake”), **Das ist unverzeihlich** (“That is unforgivable”), **etwas unverzeihlich vernachlässigen** (“to
neglect something inexcusably”). Attributive forms take normal adjective endings. Comparative **unverzeihlicher**,
superlative **am unverzeihlichsten**; these forms are possible when comparing degrees of seriousness, though the basic
meaning is often treated as absolute. The related verb is **jemandem (Dativ) etwas (Akkusativ) verzeihen** (“to forgive
someone for something”).

**Example:** *Dass er ihr Vertrauen missbraucht hat, ist für sie unverzeihlich.* — “For her, his abuse of her trust is
unforgivable.”

Another example: *Bei einer so wichtigen Prüfung war dieser Fehler unverzeihlich.* — “In such an important exam, this
mistake was inexcusable.”

**English:** **unforgivable / inexcusable** · **French:** **impardonnable / inexcusable**

Useful nuance: **unverzeihlich** expresses strong disapproval, but does not always mean that forgiveness is literally
impossible. **Ein unverzeihlicher Fehler** may simply be a very serious or avoidable mistake. **Unentschuldbar** focuses
more on the absence of a valid excuse, while **unverzeihlich** foregrounds forgiveness or moral judgment. Common
combinations include **ein unverzeihlicher Verrat** (“an unforgivable betrayal”), **eine unverzeihliche Nachlässigkeit**
(“inexcusable negligence”), and **ein unverzeihlicher Fehler** (“an unforgivable mistake”).
